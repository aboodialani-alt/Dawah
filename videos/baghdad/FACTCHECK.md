# Fact-check notes: Mongols and the fall of Baghdad

Status of every claim in the video as of 7 October 2026.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The Prophet ﷺ died in 632 | Standard date. Not re-verified here. |
| Baghdad was founded in 762 by al-Mansur | Confirmed in web sources (founded 30 July 762). |
| The Mongols under Hülegü took Baghdad in 1258 and ended Abbasid rule there | Confirmed (siege 29 Jan to 10 Feb 1258). The Abbasid line continued only as a nominal caliphate in Cairo. |
| Bukhari: small eyes, red faces, flat noses, faces like leather-covered shields | **Unverified.** sunnah.com is blocked in this environment. Confirm the wording and the narration numbers before posting. The library cites 2928 and 3590. |
| Bukhari: "shoes of hair" is a separate narration | The video says "another narration". Confirm which one. |
| Abu Dawud 4306: Banu Qantura, a city on the Tigris with a bridge, graded hasan by al-Albani | **Unverified**, same reason. |

## Weak points to know before you post

1. **The hadith names the city "Basra", not Baghdad.** The identification with Baghdad is an interpretation by some scholars. The video says "some scholars understand it as Baghdad" and shows the same note on screen. Do not drop that wording.
2. **The Bukhari hadith is about the Turks.** The Mongol armies were Mongol-Turkic, but a critic can say the Turks were well known to the Arabs. The video states this objection and answers it with the whole-picture point. It does not claim a proof.
3. **The Ibn Taymiyya quote in the library was left out** because I could not verify its wording or the reference (al-Jawab al-Sahih 2/171).
4. **The claim "the report predates the city"** (narrator Said ibn Jumhan died 136 AH, Baghdad founded 145 AH) is in the library but not in the video, because the dates are unverified.
5. **Casualty figures are omitted** on purpose. Estimates range from tens of thousands to a million.
6. The on-screen "peace be upon him" is written out because the font has no ﷺ glyph.

Before posting, open the Bukhari and Abu Dawud entries on sunnah.com yourself and check wording, numbers and grading.
