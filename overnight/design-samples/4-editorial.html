Today's lead argument · Part I

The hadith were written down in the first century

The claim that Muslims invented their Prophet's sayings two hundred years later collides with manuscripts that survive from the Companions' own generation.

The most repeated attack on the Sunnah is a dating claim: that nothing was written until al-Bukhari, born in 810, and that the gap was filled with invention. The surviving evidence tells another story.

The sahifa of Hammam ibn Munabbih, a student of Abu Hurayrah, contains 138 narrations. Set beside the musnad of Ahmad, written a century later, the texts match almost word for word. An invented corpus does not behave like this.

The Prophet ﷺ himself ordered, “Write for Abu Shah,” in Bukhari 2434, and Abdullah ibn Amr kept a written collection in his lifetime.
