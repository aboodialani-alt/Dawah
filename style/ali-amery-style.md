# Style guide: Ali Amery (@ali_amery2, TikTok)

Studied on 7 October 2026 from his 25 latest videos. 21 were analysed; the transcripts of his 8 shortest (10s to 2:51) were read in full. The transcripts are machine-made, so dialect spellings may contain errors. The 10 to 40 minute debates were not read.

## Numbers

About 61K followers, median 26K views. Best recent clips: 2 to 2.5 minute reactions (3.4x and 2.4x outliers). A 10-second clip reached 2.8x.

## What to copy

1. **Open with "السلام عليكم", then speak straight to the listener or a group.** For example "إخوان..." or "رسالة للبعض". No build-up.
2. **Read the source aloud, then react.** Pattern: "هذا الذي يقوله [المؤلف] في [الكتاب]. ماذا يقول؟ يقول: ..." Use the opponent's own source against them, and show a document on screen when possible.
3. **Formal Arabic with a little dialect.** A few colloquial words ("أكو", "شنو", "إيش", "بدك", "يا رجل", "يا حبيبي") inside formal sentences, so it sounds like a person talking.
4. **Questions that make the listener answer.** "أفعلاً هي ظروف أمنية؟" "فالذي يتبع المطي ماذا سيكون؟"
5. **Repetition as rhythm.** A short phrase said two or three times.
6. **A fork at the end.** "أنت حر: إذا كنت ... فصدّق ...، وإذا كنت ... فقل ..." The opponent's own label becomes the trap.
7. **Humor from the other side's words.** Let the absurd statement be the funny part. Add at most a light line of your own.
8. **End flat and fast.** "والسلام", or just stop. No call to action, and no punchline added at the end.
9. **Make the religion the strength, not yourself.** "القوة ليست لعلي اميري ولا لأحد فينا، إنما القوة للدين."

## What not to copy

- **Personal insults** and name-calling ("مطي", mocking someone's body or intelligence). It drives comment wars, and it clashes with a calm, respectful tone.
- **Shocking simplifications that distort the other side.** For example "البشر ابن دودة" is not what evolution claims. Every claim in a script gets fact-checked.
- **Filming real people to mock them.** Use the argument, not the person.

## Format notes

- Short clips (under 3 minutes) are the ones to learn from. The long debates are only cut down into them.
- No call to action in any of the 8 studied videos.
- Typical on-screen text: one line that states the clash ("الدكتور هيثم VS قصي") or the claim.
