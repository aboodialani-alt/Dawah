Arguments for God & against atheism

9 arguments

The Quran's case for God is mostly an appeal to what people already know: "Were they created by nothing, or were they the creators?" (52:35); "Is there any doubt about Allah, Creator of the heavens and the earth?" (14:10). This section develops that case in modern terms. It starts with the fitrah (the natural disposition to recognise God), then sets out the argument from dependent causes, the argument from fine-tuning, the arguments from consciousness and morality, and then answers the main objections: evil and suffering, free will and destiny, and Darwinism as an atheist worldview. Finally it looks at what religion, and Islam in particular, does for human wellbeing.

Argument 1 of 9

Fitrah: belief in a Creator is natural, not taught

Thesis

Children across cultures spontaneously believe nature is designed and that a powerful mind made it; atheism has to be learned and is never fully secure even in committed atheists. This is what the Quran and hadith call the fitrah.

Argument
  1. P1If belief in a Creator arises naturally in children of all cultures and persists under pressure even in atheists, it is a basic human intuition, not a cultural invention.
  2. P2Cognitive science finds exactly this: children are "intuitive theists", and even non-religious adults, when forced to answer quickly, are more likely to endorse the idea that nature was purposefully created (Järnefelt, Canfield and Kelemen 2015); physical scientists under time pressure also accept purpose-based explanations of nature (Kelemen et al. 2013).
  3. ∴Belief in God is the human default; the burden of proof lies on atheism to override it.
Evidence
  • The Quran and hadith. "So set your face to the religion, inclining to truth: the natural disposition (fitrah) of Allah upon which He created people" (30:30). "Every child is born upon the fitrah; then his parents make him a Jew, a Christian or a Magian" (Bukhari 1385). The covenant of all souls: "Am I not your Lord? They said: Yes" (7:172).
  • Justin Barrett (Born Believers, 2012): children naturally believe in a powerful, all-knowing creator and in life after death. The Oxford "Cognition, Religion and Theology" project (57 researchers, 20 countries, over 40 studies) found that people naturally believe in a god and an afterlife across cultures.
  • Olivera Petrovich's developmental studies at Oxford found young children readily attribute the origin of natural things to a creator rather than to humans; atheism is not the default.
  • Deborah Kelemen calls children "intuitive theists" because they see natural objects as made for a purpose. In "The divided mind of a disbeliever" (2015), Järnefelt, Canfield and Kelemen found that even non-religious adults, when forced to answer quickly, endorsed the view that nature was purposefully created.
  • Under stress. A small Finnish study (Lindeman et al., Helsinki, 2013; 17 participants in the first experiment) found atheists' skin-conductance arousal when reading dares to God to harm loved ones matched that of believers, and was higher than for neutral statements. Jong, Halberstadt and Bluemke (2012) found that reminders of death raised atheists' implicit (reaction-time) belief in God, though not their stated belief. The Quran: "When harm touches man, he calls upon Us, lying on his side, sitting or standing" (10:12).

فِطْرَتَ اللَّهِ الَّتِي فَطَرَ النَّاسَ عَلَيْهَا ۚ لَا تَبْدِيلَ لِخَلْقِ اللَّهِ

30:30

أَلَسْتُ بِرَبِّكُمْ ۖ قَالُوا بَلَىٰ ۛ شَهِدْنَا

7:172
Objections

"A natural belief can be false, like seeing faces in clouds."

True, so fitrah does not prove God by itself. But in philosophy a natural, universal belief is rational to hold unless defeated (like belief in other minds or the external world). It puts the burden of proof on the atheist, who must show the intuition is false.

"Evolution explains religious belief as a by-product."

Explaining how a belief arises does not show it is false; evolution also explains why we believe in mathematics. And if our belief-forming faculties were shaped only for survival, not truth, that undermines the atheist's own reasoning too.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Children also believe in Santa Claus.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Children are taught Santa, and they drop him without difficulty. Belief in a creator arises without teaching, across cultures, and atheists still show it under pressure. Santa has no such hold on anyone.

In one breath

Children everywhere naturally see the world as designed by a powerful mind; the Oxford project found it across 20 countries; atheist scientists revert to design thinking under pressure. That is the fitrah of 30:30, and it shifts the burden of proof onto atheism.

Argument 2 of 9

Dependent things need an independent cause

Thesis

Everything we observe depends on something else for its existence. A chain of dependent things cannot explain itself, even if infinitely long, so there must be an independent, necessary being: Allah.

Argument
  1. P1Everything in the universe is dependent (contingent): it exists through something else.
  2. P2A series of dependent things, however long, is itself dependent and cannot account for its own existence; an infinite regress of causes never actually produces anything.
  3. P3So there must be a being that exists independently, needing no cause.
  4. ∴A necessary, independent Creator exists, which is what the Quran calls al-Samad and al-Ghani (112:2; 35:15).
Evidence

The Quran's version. "Were they created by nothing, or were they themselves the creators? Or did they create the heavens and the earth?" (52:35-36). There are three options for something that begins to exist: it came from nothing, it made itself, or something else made it. The first two are absurd, so the third must be true.

The soldier analogy (from Ibn Taymiyya's reasoning on regress). A soldier is ordered to fire only once he receives permission from the soldier behind him, who must wait for permission from the one behind him, and so on forever. If the chain never ends in someone who can give permission without waiting, the shot is never fired. The shot was fired, so the chain ends in someone independent. Likewise, things exist, so the chain of dependence ends in a being that does not depend.

Avoid a weak version. Some argue "an infinite past is impossible because one infinite chain would have one more year than another". Mathematicians reply (via Cantor) that infinite sets can be matched one-to-one, so this argument fails. The stronger point is not about the length of time but about dependence: a series of things that each need a cause cannot supply what none of them has.

Modern cosmology dates the expansion of the observable universe from a hot, dense state to about 13.8 billion years ago; whether that was an absolute beginning is debated, though most models still point to a past boundary, which makes "it has always existed" harder for the atheist to maintain.

أَمْ خُلِقُوا مِنْ غَيْرِ شَيْءٍ أَمْ هُمُ الْخَالِقُونَ

52:35

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ أَنتُمُ الْفُقَرَاءُ إِلَى اللَّهِ ۖ وَاللَّهُ هُوَ الْغَنِيُّ الْحَمِيدُ

35:15
Objections

"Then who created God?"

The argument is about dependent things. A being that depends on nothing doesn't need a creator; asking who made it is asking for the cause of something defined as uncaused. If God had a creator, that creator would be the independent one, and the question stops there.

"Maybe the universe itself is the necessary being."

The universe is made of parts that change and depend on each other, and it began to exist. What changes and begins is dependent. The necessary being must be unchanging and without beginning, which the universe is not.

"Quantum events happen without causes."

Quantum events happen within a quantum field governed by laws; they are not things popping out of absolutely nothing. "Nothing" in physics is not nothing.

Practice

Your opponent says

“The universe could just be a brute fact with no explanation.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Then you give up explanation exactly where you most need it. Scientists never accept "brute fact" for anything else in the universe; it is special pleading to accept it for the whole. The Quran asks: were they created by nothing?

In one breath

Every dependent thing needs a cause, and a chain of dependent things, even infinite, never explains itself, like soldiers each waiting for permission. So there is an independent being. "Who made God?" misunderstands the argument: the independent being has no cause by definition.

Argument 3 of 9

The universe is fine-tuned for life

Thesis

The laws and constants of physics fall within extremely narrow ranges that permit life. Design explains this better than chance.

Argument
  1. P1The fine-tuning of the universe is due to physical necessity, chance or design.
  2. P2It is not due to necessity (the constants could have been otherwise as far as physics knows) or chance (the odds are too small).
  3. ∴It is due to design.
Evidence
  • The cosmological constant, which governs the expansion of space, is about 10^120 times smaller than naive quantum-field-theory estimates of vacuum energy, and anthropic arguments (Weinberg 1987) show it could not be more than a few hundred times its observed value without preventing galaxy formation. A slightly larger positive value would have blown matter apart before galaxies formed; a large negative value would have caused recollapse.
  • Martin Rees, the Astronomer Royal, identified six numbers (Just Six Numbers, 1999) that must be finely set for a universe with life.
  • The strength of the strong nuclear force: Rees notes the fusion efficiency 0.007 cannot be much lower than 0.006 (no atoms beyond hydrogen) or higher than 0.008 (no hydrogen left).
  • The Quran: "We did not create the heaven and the earth and what is between them in play" (21:16); "You do not see in the creation of the Most Merciful any inconsistency; look again: do you see any flaw?" (67:3).
Arabic

مَّا تَرَىٰ فِي خَلْقِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ مِن تَفَاوُتٍ ۖ فَارْجِعِ الْبَصَرَ هَلْ تَرَىٰ مِن فُطُورٍ

67:3
Objections

"The multiverse: with infinitely many universes, one like ours is inevitable."

There is no observational evidence for a multiverse, and some philosophers argue that a mechanism that produces universes would itself need fine-tuning. Positing infinitely many unobservable universes to avoid one Creator is less simple, not more.

"If the universe weren't fine-tuned, we wouldn't be here to notice (the anthropic principle)."

That explains why we observe fine-tuning, not why it exists. A man facing a firing squad of fifty marksmen who all miss shouldn't conclude "of course, otherwise I wouldn't be here to wonder"; he should conclude it was arranged.

Practice

Your opponent says

“The universe is mostly empty and hostile to life, so it isn't designed for us.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Its size and age are part of the fine-tuning: the elements of life are made in stars over billions of years. The question is why the laws allow life at all, and that needs the narrow ranges physicists describe.

In one breath

The cosmological constant, the nuclear forces and Rees' "six numbers" fall in tiny life-permitting ranges. Necessity and chance don't explain this; the multiverse is unobserved and needs tuning itself. Design is the best explanation.

Argument 4 of 9

Consciousness points beyond matter

Thesis

Matter in motion does not explain subjective experience, the unity of the self or free decision. These are what the Quran calls the ruh, the soul.

Argument
  1. P1If physical processes alone cannot explain conscious experience and the unified self, materialism is incomplete.
  2. P2Physical descriptions of the brain describe structure and function, not what it feels like to see red or feel pain (the "hard problem"); neuroscience has not found where experience is unified (the binding problem); and Penfield's stimulation experiments produced movements and sensations but not the sense of deciding; later work (Desmurget et al. 2009) found stimulation of parietal cortex can induce an urge to move, which critics cite against this point.
  3. ∴Consciousness is best explained by a non-physical soul given by God, as the Quran teaches (17:85; 15:29).
Evidence
  • The hard problem. Philosopher David Chalmers distinguished explaining brain functions (the "easy problems") from explaining why there is subjective experience at all. No physical theory explains the second.
  • Wilder Penfield, the neurosurgeon who mapped the cortex by electrical stimulation, found he could make patients move limbs, recall memories or see lights, but never make them decide or will anything. Patients would say "I didn't do that. You did". Penfield concluded that the mind is not simply a product of the brain (The Mystery of the Mind, 1975). Nobel laureate John Eccles reached a similar view.
  • The binding problem. The brain processes colour, shape, sound and motion in different regions, yet we have one unified experience. No region has been found that unifies it.
  • Psychophysical harmony. Our experiences match the physical world in an orderly way: pain goes with damage, pleasure with benefit, and our perceptions track reality. On a purely physical view there is no reason the felt quality of experience should line up with what it represents; on theism, God made mind and world to fit.
Arabic

وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الرُّوحِ ۖ قُلِ الرُّوحُ مِنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّي وَمَا أُوتِيتُم مِّنَ الْعِلْمِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

17:85
Objections

"Science will explain consciousness eventually."

That is a promissory note, not an argument. The problem is not missing data but a category gap: no amount of information about neurons tells you what it is like to be the person.

"Brain damage changes the mind, so the mind is the brain."

A damaged instrument changes the music, but the musician is not the instrument. Dependence of expression on the brain does not show identity.

Practice

Your opponent says

“The soul is just a "god of the gaps".”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

The argument doesn't rely on a gap in data but on a difference in kind: physics describes quantities, structures and causes, while experience is qualitative and first-person. That is a principled limit, not a temporary gap.

In one breath

Physical descriptions don't explain subjective experience; Penfield could trigger movement and memory but never a decision; nothing in the brain binds experience into one self. The Quran's ruh fits the evidence.

Argument 5 of 9

Objective morality needs God

Thesis

If there is no God, there is no objective moral standard, only preferences and conventions. Most people, including atheists, believe some things are really wrong. So that belief points to God.

Argument
  1. P1If God does not exist, objective moral values (true regardless of opinion) do not exist.
  2. P2Objective moral values do exist: torturing children for fun is wrong whatever anyone thinks.
  3. ∴God exists.
Evidence
  • Nietzsche, an atheist philosopher who saw the consequences of atheism clearly, warned that if you abandon Christian belief you cannot keep Christian morality: "when one gives up the Christian faith, one pulls the right to Christian morality out from under one's feet" (Twilight of the Idols).
  • Hume's is-ought gap. From facts about what is (evolution, pleasure, harm) you cannot derive what ought to be. Secular moral systems choose a starting value (harm, wellbeing) without being able to justify why we are obliged to pursue it.
  • Where secular ethics leads. Peter Singer, a leading utilitarian, has defended infanticide of severely disabled newborns and argued in his 2001 essay 'Heavy Petting' that sex with animals is not always abusive and not necessarily seriously wrong, conclusions most people find monstrous but which follow from his premises. In a debate with Mohammed Hijab, the Norwegian philosopher Lars Gule struggled to say incest between consenting adults is objectively wrong.
  • The Quran grounds morality in God's wisdom and in the fitrah: "and [by] the soul and He who proportioned it, and inspired it with its wickedness and its righteousness" (91:7-8).
Arabic

وَنَفْسٍ وَمَا سَوَّاهَا ۝ فَأَلْهَمَهَا فُجُورَهَا وَتَقْوَاهَا

91:7-8
Objections

"Atheists can be moral."

Of course; the argument is not about whether atheists behave well but about what makes moral claims true. An atheist can know murder is wrong; he can't explain why it is objectively wrong.

"Euthyphro: is something good because God commands it, or does God command it because it's good?"

Neither: God's commands flow from His nature, which is good and wise (al-Hakim). Good is not an arbitrary decree nor a standard above God; it is grounded in who He is.

"Morality evolved because it helped survival."

Then it is useful, not true. If evolution had made us value cruelty, cruelty would be "moral" in the same sense. That is exactly the relativism the atheist wanted to avoid.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Morality is just about reducing harm; you don't need God for that.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Why is harm bad objectively? If the answer is "because we don't like it", that's preference, not morality. And whose harm counts: animals, embryos, future people? Utilitarians like Singer reach conclusions most people find horrifying from that same principle.

In one breath

Without God there is no ground for objective morality, as Nietzsche admitted, and Hume's is-ought gap shows facts alone can't create obligation. Since some things really are wrong, God exists.

Argument 6 of 9

The problem of evil and suffering

Thesis

Suffering does not disprove an All-Wise, All-Merciful God. The logical problem has been abandoned even by atheist philosophers, and the evidential problem assumes we can see what an infinitely wise God sees.

Argument
  1. P1Evil disproves God only if there could be no wise reason for God to permit it.
  2. P2Islam gives many reasons (testing, growth in virtue, expiation of sins, human free choice, and a final perfect justice), and we are not in a position to know that no reason exists for any particular case.
  3. ∴Evil does not disprove God.
Evidence
  • The logical version is widely regarded as defeated. Alvin Plantinga's free-will defence showed there is no contradiction between God and evil. J.L. Mackie, who made the logical argument famous, conceded that "the problem of evil does not, after all, show that the central doctrines of theism are logically inconsistent with one another" (The Miracle of Theism, p. 154).
  • Wisdom. A surgeon cuts, a parent refuses a child sweets; a child cannot see the reason. "Perhaps you hate a thing and it is good for you… Allah knows and you do not know" (2:216). The story of Moses and al-Khidr (18:65-82) makes this point: each apparent harm had a hidden good.
  • Testing and growth. "We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger and loss of wealth and lives and fruits; but give good tidings to the patient" (2:155). Patience, courage and compassion cannot exist without hardship.
  • Expiation. "No fatigue, illness, worry, grief or harm befalls a Muslim, even a thorn prick, except that Allah expiates some of his sins by it" (Bukhari 5641). "When Allah loves a people He tests them" (Tirmidhi 2396); the prophets were tested most.
  • Human responsibility. "Corruption has appeared on land and sea because of what people's hands have earned, so that He may let them taste some of what they did, that perhaps they will return" (30:41).
  • Justice deferred, not denied. This world is temporary; every wrong will be judged. Atheism tells victims of injustice that their suffering simply ends in nothing.
  • No pure evil. Ibn Taymiyya: Allah creates nothing that is evil in every respect; what is evil from one angle serves wisdom from another. Allah wills some things by His creative will (irada kawniyya) that He does not love by His legislative will (irada shar'iyya): "He does not approve of disbelief for His servants" (39:7).
Arabic

وَعَسَىٰ أَن تَكْرَهُوا شَيْئًا وَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ ۖ وَعَسَىٰ أَن تُحِبُّوا شَيْئًا وَهُوَ شَرٌّ لَّكُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

2:216
Objections

"What about animal suffering or children with cancer, where no one grows?"

We often cannot see the reason, and Islam does not claim we always can. Robin Collins' analogy: you can't conclude there is no snake in a garden just because you can't see one, if snakes are the kind of thing you'd expect not to see. If God exists, we would expect many of His reasons to be beyond us. Children of believers who die are in Paradise (see the Prophet's dream of Abraham surrounded by children, Bukhari 7047); scholars differ on the children of non-Muslims, and every creature will be compensated (Muslim 2582).

"An all-powerful God could achieve the goods without the suffering."

Not if the goods (patience, courage, forgiveness, sacrifice) logically require hardship. Even omnipotence cannot make courage without danger.

Practice

Your opponent says

“If God were good, He'd stop the suffering of innocent children.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

You are assuming this life is the whole story. Islam says it is a short test followed by eternal justice and mercy; children who die go to Paradise. Atheism has the bigger problem: on atheism, that child's suffering means nothing and is never put right.

In one breath

Mackie himself conceded the logical problem fails. Suffering serves testing, growth, expiation and human freedom; we can't expect to see every reason of an All-Wise God; and only theism offers final justice to victims.

Argument 7 of 9

Free will and divine decree (qadar)

Thesis

God's knowledge and decree do not remove human choice. Knowing an act is not causing it, and God creates the act while the human being chooses it.

Argument
  1. P1The objection says: if God knows and decrees what I will do, I am not free.
  2. P2Knowledge is not causation: a teacher who knows a lazy student will fail does not make him fail. And on the Islamic view, God creates the power and the act, while the person chooses and owns the choice (kasb).
  3. ∴Divine decree and human responsibility are compatible, as the Quran affirms both together.
Evidence
  • Both affirmed. "Whoever wills, let him believe; and whoever wills, let him disbelieve" (18:29). "And you do not will except that Allah wills, Lord of the worlds" (81:29).
  • Ibn al-Qayyim: two independent, complete agents for one act is impossible; but a dependent agent acting through power given by the Creator is not. The person truly chooses; God creates and permits.
  • Coercion vs causation. The objection confuses being the ultimate cause of everything with forcing someone against his will. We experience our choices as our own, and God judges us by them.
Objections

"If God already wrote my fate, why should I act?"

The Companions asked the Prophet ﷺ this, and he said: "Act, for everyone is eased toward what he was created for" (Bukhari 4949). The decree includes the means; you don't know your decree, so you act.

Practice

Your opponent says

“If God knew I'd go to Hell, how is it fair to punish me?”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

God's knowledge doesn't make you choose; it records what you freely choose. A recording of a crime doesn't cause the crime. You are judged by what you did with real choices.

In one breath

Knowledge isn't causation; God creates the act and the person chooses it (kasb); the Quran affirms human will (18:29) and God's will (81:29) together; and the Prophet ﷺ said "act, for each is eased toward what he was created for".

Argument 8 of 9

Darwinism as an atheist worldview

Thesis

Evolution, even if true in full, does not remove the need for a Creator, and "Darwinism" as an atheist philosophy goes far beyond the science. Mainstream biologists themselves now debate the adequacy of the standard theory, and the origin of life remains unexplained.

Argument
  1. P1A scientific theory of how living things change says nothing about why there is a universe, why its laws permit life, or how life began.
  2. P2Darwinism is often presented as a complete atheist worldview, yet biologists at the Royal Society have discussed its "explanatory deficits", the origin of life is unsolved, and its history includes frauds and social harms.
  3. ∴Darwinism does not support atheism, and its use as a worldview is philosophy, not science.
Evidence
  • A worldview. Richard Dawkins: Darwin "made it possible to be an intellectually fulfilled atheist". Julian Huxley promoted "evolutionary humanism" as a substitute religion; Michael Ruse wrote Darwinism as Religion (2016). Lynn Margulis predicted neo-Darwinism would be judged "a minor twentieth-century religious sect".
  • Debate within biology. At the Royal Society's 2016 meeting on "New trends in evolutionary biology", Gerd Müller spoke of the 'explanatory deficits' of the Modern Synthesis, especially the origin of complexity and novelty, and argued for extending it
  • Origin of life. The Miller-Urey experiment (1953) produced some amino acids and tar, not life. No accepted pathway from chemistry to the first self-replicating cell exists; chemist James Tour has criticised overstated claims. A US$10 million prize offered by businessman Perry Marshall (Evolution 2.0 Prize) for explaining the natural origin of the genetic code remains unclaimed, though it is a private, non-scientific prize
  • Frauds and errors. Piltdown Man (a human skull with an orangutan jaw) was accepted for 40 years until exposed in 1953; "Nebraska Man" (1922) was built from a peccary's tooth and was retracted within five years; anthropologist Reiner Protsch was found in 2005 to have falsified dates of fossils. Textbook photos of peppered moths were staged; biologist Jerry Coyne said learning this was like "finding out that Santa Claus is not real". (Later research did support natural selection in moths, so use this example only to show how textbooks overstated evidence.)
  • Social Darwinism and eugenics drew on Darwinian thinking; Darwin himself wrote in The Descent of Man that men attain "a higher eminence" than women.
  • Islam's stance. Scientific theories are revisable. Muslim scholars hold a range of views on the details of biological evolution; most Muslim scholars hold that Allah created Adam and that whatever processes exist are His creation. The Quran's challenge is not to biology but to the claim that nature explains itself.
Objections

"Evolution is a fact; you're anti-science."

The argument doesn't depend on rejecting all evolution. It shows that evolution, true or not, can't explain the universe, its fine-tuning or the origin of life, and that turning it into an atheist worldview is philosophy.

"Frauds were exposed by science, which shows it works."

Agreed, and that is why we shouldn't treat current models as final truth. Science corrects itself; worldviews built on its present state should be modest.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Evolution shows design is unnecessary: natural selection explains complexity.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Natural selection needs self-replicating life to start with, which it can't explain, and works within laws that are fine-tuned. Even the Royal Society meeting discussed unexplained origins of novelty. A process doesn't make a designer unnecessary; a car factory's robots don't disprove engineers.

In one breath

Evolution can't explain the universe, its laws or the origin of life. Biologists at the Royal Society debated the standard theory's "explanatory deficits", the origin-of-life prize is unclaimed, and the field's history includes Piltdown and Nebraska Man.

Argument 9 of 9

Religion, and Islam in particular, makes for healthier lives

Wellbeing data show benefit, not truth. Use this to answer "religion poisons everything", not as a proof of God.

  • Family and sexual behaviour. A study in the American Sociological Review (77:5, 2012) found Muslims the least likely to report extramarital sex, and Hindus and Muslims less likely than Christians and Jews to report premarital sex.
  • Life satisfaction. A German study (Erdinger-Schons, 2019) found Muslims scored highest on beliefs in oneness, and that oneness beliefs predicted higher life satisfaction
  • Substance use. Religiosity is consistently linked with less drug and alcohol abuse, and the Islamic ban is associated with much lower alcohol consumption in Muslim societies (see Miracles & the wisdom of the law).
  • Body image. Research by Viren Swami and colleagues associated wearing the hijab with more positive body image.

"Scientific errors" answered

6 arguments

Critics often claim the Quran or hadith contain scientific mistakes: a flat earth, the sun setting in a muddy spring, semen produced between the backbone and ribs, contradictory creation days, and Greek embryology. Each claim depends on misreading the Arabic, ignoring how classical scholars understood the text, or holding the Quran to a standard the critic's own scripture fails. The general principle: the Quran describes nature as people see it (phenomenological language, as when we say "sunrise") and does not teach a cosmology that science has refuted. Its classical commentators held the earth to be a sphere centuries before Europe's flat-earth myths.

Argument 1 of 6

The Quran and Muslim scholars held the earth to be round

Thesis

Classical Muslim scholars reported a consensus that the earth and heavens are spherical, and the Quran's language of night "wrapping" day and bodies "swimming" in orbits fits a round world.

Argument
  1. P1If the Quran taught a flat earth, its earliest and greatest interpreters would have read it that way.
  2. P2Leading scholars across schools (Ibn al-Munadi, Ibn Hazm, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn Kathir, Ibn al-Qayyim) reported scholarly consensus that the earth is spherical, and cited the Quran for it.
  3. ∴The Quran does not teach a flat earth.
Evidence
  • The consensus. Ibn Taymiyya says the scholars agree the earth is spherical, citing the Hanbali scholar Abu al-Husayn ibn al-Munadi (d. 336 AH) and Ibn Hazm, and adds that only an ignorant person denies it (Majmu' al-Fatawa 5/150; 6/586-587). Ibn al-Jawzi notes the sun rises in the east before the west, which requires a sphere. Ibn Kathir (al-Bidaya 1/31) and Ibn al-Qayyim (the heavens surround the earth like an egg) agree. Al-Ghazali, al-Razi and Ibn Khaldun took sphericity for granted.
  • "He wraps the night over the day" (39:5). The verb yukawwir means to wind or wrap, as a turban is wound around the head; Ibn Hazm took it to imply a round earth.
  • "Each swims in an orbit (falak)" (21:33; 36:40). Ibn Abbas compared the falak to the whorl of a spindle (Tabari), something round and turning.
  • "Spread out" (13:3; 79:30). Abu Hayyan explains that a huge sphere appears flat to someone standing on it (al-Bahr al-Muhit 5/355). That is why the earth is described as spread out: from where we stand, it is.
Arabic

يُكَوِّرُ اللَّيْلَ عَلَى النَّهَارِ وَيُكَوِّرُ النَّهَارَ عَلَى اللَّيْلِ

وَهُوَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ وَالشَّمْسَ وَالْقَمَرَ ۖ كُلٌّ فِي فَلَكٍ يَسْبَحُونَ

21:33
Objections

"Some Muslims today believe in a flat earth, and al-Suyuti did."

Even al-Suyuti acknowledged the reports of consensus; a few individuals' views don't define the text. The mainstream classical position, stated by scholars from every school, is a spherical earth.

Practice

Your opponent says

“The Quran says the earth is "spread out like a carpet" (71:19), so it's flat.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

A carpet describes how the ground is laid out for us to walk on, not the planet's shape; Abu Hayyan explains a vast sphere appears flat to those on it. The scholars who knew these verses best agreed the earth is round.

In one breath

Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn Hazm, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Kathir and Ibn al-Qayyim reported consensus that the earth is round. "Wrapping" night around day (39:5) uses the root of "ball", and "spread out" describes how a huge sphere looks from its surface.

Argument 2 of 6

"The sun setting in a muddy spring" (18:86)

Thesis

The verse says Dhul-Qarnayn "found it" setting in a dark spring, describing what he saw, as we say "the sun sets in the sea". The hadith wording that seems to make it literal comes through a weak link.

Argument
  1. P1A narrative describing what a person saw is not a claim about astronomy.
  2. P2The verse says "he found it setting (wajadaha taghrubu)" in a spring, describing Dhul-Qarnayn's view at the edge of the land; the classical commentators read it that way.
  3. ∴18:86 is not a scientific error.
Evidence
  • "He found it". The verb wajada reports perception from Dhul-Qarnayn's viewpoint. The commentators said this explicitly: Ibn Kathir says he saw it as if setting in the sea, as anyone at the shore sees; al-Shinqiti, the Jalalayn and al-Sa'di say the same.
  • Ordinary language. Astronomers and NASA still speak of "sunset" and "sunrise". No one accuses them of geocentrism.
  • The hadith. A report of Abu Dharr has the Prophet ﷺ say the sun "sets in a hot spring". That wording comes only through Sufyan ibn Husayn, whose narrations from al-Zuhri are weak and who is confused here; five stronger narrators (al-A'mash, Yunus ibn 'Ubayd, Musa ibn al-Musayyab, Harun ibn Sa'd and 'Abd al-A'la) narrate from Ibrahim al-Taymi without the addition. Al-Bazzar noted the wording is singular.
  • The Bible: "The sun rises, and the sun goes down, and hastens to the place where it rises" (Eccl 1:5); it "comes out like a bridegroom… its rising is from the end of the heavens and its circuit to the end of them" (Ps 19:5-6).

حَتَّىٰ إِذَا بَلَغَ مَغْرِبَ الشَّمْسِ وَجَدَهَا تَغْرُبُ فِي عَيْنٍ حَمِئَةٍ

"Why would God report a mistaken perception?"

Reporting what someone saw is not endorsing a false belief; the Quran is telling a story from his viewpoint, and the same Quran says the sun "runs to a resting place" and swims in its own orbit (36:38-40).

That's exactly what wajadaha does: "he found it setting". The commentators understood it as appearance a thousand years ago. Your own Bible says the sun "hastens to its place".

Argument 3 of 6

"Between the backbone and the ribs" (86:6-7)

Thesis

The verse does not say semen is made between the backbone and ribs. Its subject can be the human being who emerges, and the surah's theme is creation and resurrection.

Argument
  1. P1A claimed error must be what the verse actually says.
  2. P286:6-7 says man was created "from a gushing fluid that emerges (yakhruj) from between the backbone and the ribs". Several classical scholars read the subject of "emerges" as man himself (v. 5), emerging from his mother, between her back and front; and yakhruj is the Quran's word for living things coming out.
  3. ∴The verse does not make the anatomical claim critics attribute to it.
Evidence
  • The grammar. Verse 5: "Let man consider from what he was created." Verse 6: "He was created from a fluid, gushing." Verse 7: "It/he emerges (yakhruj) from between the backbone and the ribs." The pronoun in yakhruj can refer to the fluid or to man. Ibn 'Atiyya (al-Muharrar al-Wajiz 5/465), al-Mahdawi, Makki and al-Qurtubi record the view that it refers to man.
  • Usage. The Quran uses yakhruj for life emerging: "He brings the living out of the dead" (30:19); "then you will be brought out (takhrujun)" from the earth at resurrection (30:25).
  • The theme. M.A.S. Abdel Haleem shows the surah is about resurrection: the very next verse says "Indeed, He is able to bring him back" (86:8). The argument is: the God who brought you out once can bring you out again.
  • The Quran's embryology is described in stages: nutfa (drop), 'alaqa (clinging thing), mudgha (chewed lump), bones, flesh clothing the bones, then "another creation" (23:12-14).
Arabic

خُلِقَ مِن مَّاءٍ دَافِقٍ ۝ يَخْرُجُ مِن بَيْنِ الصُّلْبِ وَالتَّرَائِبِ

86:6-7
Objections

"Most classical commentators said it refers to the fluid."

Many did, and they read sulb and tara'ib as the man's back and the woman's chest, interpreting with the knowledge of their time. The Quran's wording allows the other reading, which classical scholars also recorded, and the critic needs to show the text must mean his reading.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Semen comes from the testes, not between the backbone and ribs.”

The verse doesn't say semen is produced there; it says "it/he emerges from between", and classical scholars recorded that the subject is man, emerging between the mother's back and front. Embryologically, the testes also originate in the abdomen near the kidneys before descending.

In one breath

86:7's verb can refer to man emerging (Ibn 'Atiyya, Qurtubi), yakhruj is used for life coming forth, and the surah is about resurrection: "He is able to bring him back" (86:8).

Argument 4 of 6

The Quran did not copy Greek embryology

Thesis

Greek medical works were not yet translated into Arabic in the Prophet's time, though Greek-trained physicians such as al-Harith ibn Kalada were known in Arabia. Greek medicine was full of errors the Quran does not contain. A copier cannot filter out every error without knowing the truth.

Argument
  1. P1If the Quran copied Galen or Hippocrates, it would have needed access to their works and would reproduce their mistakes.
  2. P2Greek medical works were translated into Arabic mainly in the Abbasid period (8th-9th centuries), and they taught errors such as the fetus being formed from menstrual blood and seed coming from every part of the body. The Quran contains none of these.
  3. ∴The Quran's embryology was not copied from the Greeks.
Evidence
  • No access. The Prophet ﷺ was unlettered (29:48), and the great translation movement in Baghdad came over a century after him.
  • Greek errors. Galen and Aristotle taught that the fetus forms from menstrual blood shaped by semen. Hippocrates taught pangenesis, that seed is drawn from every part of the body. The four humours, the "rete mirabile" network in the human brain, and the screaming mandrake legend (Pliny and medieval herbals) were all part of Greek medicine.
  • The Quran says the human being is created from a mixed drop (nutfa amshaj, 76:2) and describes stages, with none of these errors.
  • Women's contribution. Arabs commonly believed the mother was only a vessel. The hadith describes the woman's fluid as contributing to the child and to resemblance (Muslim 311; Bukhari 3329), correcting that belief.
Objections

"Galen also had stages, so the Quran copied the idea of stages."

Stages are observable in miscarriages; describing them is not copying. What would show copying is reproducing Galen's specific theory, and the Quran doesn't.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Christian doctors in Arabia like al-Harith ibn Kalada knew Greek medicine and taught Muhammad.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

There's no evidence al-Harith taught the Prophet anything, and the reports about his training are late and thin. More importantly, if Greek medicine had been the source, its errors would show up. They don't.

In one breath

Greek medicine reached Arabic in the 8th-9th centuries, and it taught that the fetus forms from menstrual blood and seed comes from every body part. The Quran has none of these errors, which a copyist couldn't avoid.

Argument 5 of 6

Six days or eight? (41:9-12)

Thesis

The "four days" of 41:10 include the two days of 41:9, so the total is six, consistent with the rest of the Quran.

Argument
  1. P1The Quran elsewhere says creation took six days (7:54; 50:38).
  2. P2In 41:9-12: the earth in two days; then mountains, blessings and provisions "in four days", which the commentators read as completing four days including the first two; then the heavens in two days. That is 2 + 2 + 2 = 6.
  3. ∴There is no contradiction.
Evidence
  • The Arabic "in four days" (fi arba'ati ayyam) is a running total, as when one says "I reached the city in ten days and Mecca in fifteen", where the fifteen includes the ten. Al-Zamakhshari and other commentators read it this way.
  • Thumma ("then") in 41:11 does not always mean sequence in time; it can mean "moreover" or ordering in speech. In 6:151-154, after listing commands to Muhammad's ﷺ community, the Quran says "then We gave Moses the Book", although Moses came earlier. In 7:11: "We created you, then We shaped you, then We told the angels to bow to Adam."
Objections

"That's an ad hoc fix."

It is the classical reading, given long before modern critics raised the objection, and Arabic usage supports running totals. A sentence must be read in its language's idiom.

Practice

Your opponent says

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Only if "four days" is additional. Arabic often states cumulative totals; the commentators centuries ago read it as four days in all for the earth. And thumma in 6:154 shows "then" can order the speech, not the events.

In one breath

The "four days" in 41:10 includes the first two, so the total is six; thumma can order speech rather than time (6:154; 7:11).

Argument 6 of 6

The seven earths (65:12)

Thesis

"Seven earths like them" has several classical readings: seven layers of the earth or seven earth-like worlds with creatures, as Ibn Abbas suggested. Neither is a scientific error, and many of the colourful details critics cite are weak Israelite narrations.

  2. P265:12 says only that Allah created seven heavens "and of the earth the like of them". The early interpretations differ, and the legends of the fish Nun and the mountain Qaf are weak or from Jewish and Christian lore (Isra'iliyyat).
  3. ∴The verse makes no claim science has disproved.
Evidence
  • The verse. "Allah is He who created seven heavens and of the earth the like of them; the command descends among them" (65:12).
  • Hadith. "Whoever wrongfully takes a hand-span of land will have it hung around his neck from seven earths" (Bukhari 2452; Muslim 1610).
  • Ibn Abbas is reported to have said that in each earth there is a prophet like yours, and that "if I told you its explanation you would reject it". Qatada said there are creatures and commands in every earth. Al-Bayhaqi said the chain is sound but the text is unsupported (shadh), and Ibn Kathir thought it may be from Isra'iliyyat
  • Weak details. Ibn Kathir graded weak the stories of the earth on a fish or a bull and of the mountain Qaf; they are not Quran or authentic hadith.

"Geology doesn't have exactly seven layers."

Refuting agnosticism: from a Creator to His attributes

7 arguments

The agnostic says: "Maybe there is a God, maybe not; we can't know." Some agnostics accept a "first cause" but say we can know nothing about it, so it might as well be an unconscious force. This section answers both. First it shows that agnosticism is not a neutral resting place: it has to be argued for, and the arguments fail. Then it builds, step by step, from the existence of an independent, necessary being (see Arguments for God) to its attributes: one, eternal, self-sufficient, not physical, living, knowing, willing, powerful, wise and merciful. Finally it argues that such a God would communicate with us, which brings the question to prophethood. The classical sources are Ibn Taymiyya, al-Ghazali (al-Iqtisad fi al-I'tiqad), Ibn Rushd's arguments from providence and invention, and in modern philosophy Edward Feser (Five Proofs of the Existence of God) and Richard Swinburne.

Argument 1 of 7

Agnosticism is a claim, and it carries a burden

Thesis

"We can't know whether God exists" is not the absence of a position. It is a claim about knowledge that needs evidence, and the agnostic usually offers none.

Argument
  1. P1Saying "no one can know X" is a positive claim about the limits of knowledge, and needs justification.
  2. P2To justify it, the agnostic must show that the arguments for God fail and that no future argument could succeed, which he cannot do; usually he simply hasn't engaged with the arguments.
  3. P3Where the evidence points one way (dependence, order, consciousness, morality, the fitrah), suspending judgement is not neutral; it is ignoring evidence.
  4. ∴Agnosticism is not a default; it has the burden to show the evidence is balanced, and it rarely meets it.
Evidence
  • Two kinds of agnosticism. "I don't know" (personal agnosticism) is an honest report, and the cure is to examine the arguments. "No one can know" (strong agnosticism) is a sweeping claim about all possible evidence, and it is self-undermining if pushed: how does the agnostic know that no one can know?
  • The stakes. A person who says "maybe there is a God who will judge me" and then stops investigating is not being cautious; he is being careless about the most important question he could ask. The Quran addresses this directly: "Do they not reflect within themselves? Allah did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them except in truth and for an appointed term" (30:8).
  • The fitrah. Belief in a Creator is the natural default (see Arguments for God); the agnostic is the one departing from it.
  • Doubt that is not reasoned. The Quran describes people who "follow nothing but assumption, and assumption avails nothing against the truth" (53:28), and calls on them to "bring your proof if you are truthful" (2:111).
Arabic

أَمْ خُلِقُوا مِنْ غَيْرِ شَيْءٍ أَمْ هُمُ الْخَالِقُونَ ۝ أَمْ خَلَقُوا السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ ۚ بَل لَّا يُوقِنُونَ

52:35-36
Objections

"I just lack belief; I'm not claiming anything."

Then you are not an agnostic about the evidence but someone who hasn't evaluated it. The right response is to look at it, which this library sets out.

"Both sides have good arguments, so I suspend judgement."

That requires showing they are evenly balanced. Ask which arguments for God the agnostic finds weak and why. Usually the conversation shows the "balance" was assumed.

Practice

Your opponent says

“I'm agnostic: you can't prove God, and I can't disprove Him, so it's 50/50.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Not knowing which side is right doesn't make the odds 50/50; it means you haven't weighed the evidence. Let's weigh it: do you think things that depend on other things can explain themselves? If not, something independent exists. Then we can ask what it must be like.

In one breath

"No one can know" is a claim needing proof, and it undermines itself. Suspending judgement is only rational if the evidence is balanced, and the agnostic must show that. Given the stakes, not investigating is not caution.

Argument 2 of 7

Why the first cause must be one

Thesis

There cannot be two independent, necessary beings. If there were two, something would have to distinguish them, and whatever distinguishes them would make them composite or dependent. So the necessary being is one.

Argument
  1. P1A necessary being exists (see Arguments for God: dependent things require an independent cause).
  2. P2If there were two necessary beings, they would share necessary existence and differ in something else.
  3. P3The thing that distinguishes each would make it composite (necessary existence plus a distinguishing feature), and a composite depends on its parts, so it would not be truly independent.
  4. P4Also, two independent wills could conflict; if one could block the other, the blocked one is not all-powerful; if neither can, neither is.
  5. ∴The necessary being is one, without partner.
Evidence
  • The Quran's version. "Had there been in the heavens and earth gods besides Allah, they would both have been ruined" (21:22). "Allah has not taken any son, nor has there been with Him any god; then each god would have taken what it created, and some of them would have overpowered others" (23:91). This is the burhan al-tamanu' (argument from mutual hindrance) developed by the theologians: two all-powerful wills either agree necessarily (so neither is independent) or can conflict (so one or both is limited).
  • The philosophical version argues that what is purely necessary, with no distinction between what it is and that it is, cannot be multiplied: there is nothing by which two such beings could differ.
  • Observation supports it. The universe runs on one consistent set of laws; there is no sign of rival designers.
Arabic

لَوْ كَانَ فِيهِمَا آلِهَةٌ إِلَّا اللَّهُ لَفَسَدَتَا

21:22

مَا اتَّخَذَ اللَّهُ مِن وَلَدٍ وَمَا كَانَ مَعَهُ مِنْ إِلَٰهٍ ۚ إِذًا لَّذَهَبَ كُلُّ إِلَٰهٍ بِمَا خَلَقَ وَلَعَلَا بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ

23:91
Objections

"Two gods could just agree."

If they agree because each could not act otherwise, neither is free and all-powerful; if they agree by choice, each could have willed differently and one of them would then be overpowered. Either way, a being that could be blocked is not the ultimate cause.

"Doesn't this argument work against the Trinity too?"

Yes, which is one reason Christianity must insist the three are one being. The Quran's argument shows that ultimate reality is one, and the problems with saying that one being is three persons are covered in How the Trinity developed.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Maybe there are many first causes, a committee of gods.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Then each would need something that makes it different from the others, so each would be composite and dependent on its parts, and none would be the ultimate, independent cause. And as 21:22 says, rival ultimate wills would mean a world that could be pulled apart, but we see one consistent order.

In one breath

Two necessary beings would need something to distinguish them, making them composite and dependent; and two all-powerful wills could block each other (21:22; 23:91). So the necessary being is one.

Argument 3 of 7

Eternal, self-sufficient and not physical

Thesis

Whatever exists necessarily cannot begin or end, cannot depend on anything, and cannot be made of parts. So it is eternal, self-sufficient (al-Samad, al-Ghani) and not material, since matter is composite, changing and dependent.

Argument
  1. P1A necessary being cannot fail to exist, so it has no beginning and no end.
  2. P2It depends on nothing, otherwise it would not be the ultimate explanation.
  3. P3Anything physical is composed of parts and exists in space and time, and so depends on its parts and on the conditions of space and time.
  4. ∴The necessary being is eternal, self-sufficient, and not physical; it is not "inside" the universe and is not the universe.
Evidence
  • The Quran. "He is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden" (57:3); "Allah, al-Samad" (112:2), the one on whom all depend while He depends on none; "O people, you are the ones in need of Allah, and Allah is the Self-Sufficient (al-Ghani), the Praiseworthy" (35:15).
  • Why the universe can't be it. The universe began (standard cosmology), is made of parts, and changes. Each of these marks it as dependent.
  • Why "energy" or "the laws of physics" can't be it. Energy is a measurable quantity of something physical; laws describe how physical things behave; neither explains why physical things exist.
  • "Nothing is like Him" (42:11). Being uncaused and non-physical, God is not one more thing among things.
Arabic

هُوَ الْأَوَّلُ وَالْآخِرُ وَالظَّاهِرُ وَالْبَاطِنُ ۖ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

57:3

اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ

112:2
Objections

"Maybe the first cause is a physical quantum vacuum."

A quantum vacuum has properties, obeys laws and fluctuates; it is something with parts and conditions, so it needs an explanation too. It is not "nothing" and it is not independent.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Why can't the universe itself be eternal and necessary?”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Because it has a beginning by the best cosmology, is made of parts that depend on each other, and is constantly changing. A necessary being can't begin, can't depend on parts, and can't be different from what it must be.

In one breath

A necessary being can't begin or end, depends on nothing, and can't be made of parts; the universe, matter and energy fail all three. So the first cause is eternal, self-sufficient and beyond the physical (57:3; 112:2; 42:11).

Argument 4 of 7

Living, knowing, willing and powerful

Thesis

The first cause chose to create this particular universe out of many possibilities, so it has will; to choose, it must know; to bring the universe into being, it must have power; and what knows, wills and acts is living. An "unconscious force" cannot explain the specific universe we have.

Argument
  1. P1The universe is one of many possible universes: it could have had different laws, constants, sizes and contents, or not existed at all.
  2. P2Something must explain why this possibility was actualised rather than another; a cause that acts by blind necessity would produce its effect eternally and uniformly, and could not explain a particular, specific, beginning universe.
  3. P3Selecting one option among many is what a will does (takhsis, particularisation), and willing requires knowing what is willed.
  4. P4Producing the effect requires power; and a being with knowledge, will and power is living.
  5. ∴The first cause is living, knowing, willing and powerful: a personal Creator, not an impersonal force.
Evidence
  • The argument from particularisation (dalil al-takhsis) of the Muslim theologians, developed by al-Ghazali and others: the world has a specific size, shape, time of beginning and order. Nothing in the world's nature requires those specifics, so a chooser specified them.
  • Ibn Rushd's two arguments (al-Kashf 'an Manahij al-Adilla): the argument from providence (dalil al-'inaya), that the world is suited to life and human needs, which points to a knowing purpose; and the argument from invention (dalil al-ikhtira'), that living things and their properties are brought into being, pointing to a maker.
  • Knowledge. "Would He not know, the One who created, while He is the Subtle, the Acquainted?" (67:14). The fine-tuning of the universe for life (see Arguments for God) is a mark of knowledge.
  • The beginning. A cause that acted by necessity, without will, would have its effect as long as it existed, so an eternal impersonal cause would produce an eternal universe. A universe that began points to a cause that chose when to act. William Lane Craig puts this as the argument that the first cause must be personal.
  • Life. Knowledge, will and power are attributes only of a living being: "Allah, there is no god but He, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of all" (2:255).
Arabic

أَلَا يَعْلَمُ مَنْ خَلَقَ وَهُوَ اللَّطِيفُ الْخَبِيرُ

67:14

إِنَّمَا أَمْرُهُ إِذَا أَرَادَ شَيْئًا أَن يَقُولَ لَهُ كُن فَيَكُونُ

36:82
Objections

"Maybe the universe is the only possible universe, so no choice was needed."

Nothing in physics requires these constants or this universe; physicists treat the constants as free parameters, which is why fine-tuning is discussed at all. Claiming this is the only possible universe is an assertion with no support.

"An unconscious process like evolution produces order without a mind."

Evolution operates inside a universe already governed by laws and already containing self-replicating life; it can't explain why there is a universe with laws suited to life at all.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Fine, there's a first cause, but it could be an unconscious force.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

An unconscious force acting by necessity would produce its effect always and the same way, so it couldn't explain a universe that began at a point and has these particular laws among many possible ones. Choosing one possibility over others is what a will does, and a will needs knowledge. That's a living, personal Creator.

In one breath

The universe is one specific option among many and had a beginning; a blind cause would produce an eternal, uniform effect. Choosing requires will and knowledge, acting requires power, and these require life (2:255; 36:82; 67:14).

Argument 5 of 7

Perfect, wise and merciful

Thesis

The source of all existence and all perfections must itself have those perfections without limit and without the defects that come with them in creatures. This gives the attributes of perfection: wisdom, mercy, justice, and freedom from every deficiency.

Argument
  1. P1A cause cannot give what it lacks: whatever perfections exist in creation (life, knowledge, power, beauty, goodness, mercy) come ultimately from the first cause.
  2. P2The first cause, being independent and unlimited, has these perfections in the highest degree and without the defects they have in creatures (ignorance, weakness, need).
  3. P3Its acts show wisdom (order, purpose, fitting means to ends) and mercy (provision, beauty, the capacity for joy and love).
  4. ∴The Creator is perfect: wise, merciful, just, and free from every defect. This is the Islamic principle that Allah has "the most beautiful names" (7:180) and the "highest description" (16:60).
Evidence
  • Ibn Taymiyya's principle (qiyas al-awla, the argument a fortiori). Every perfection found in a creature, without any defect attached to it, belongs to the Creator more fittingly; every defect from which creatures are free, the Creator is more fittingly free of. The creature's knower implies the Creator knows; the creature's merciful parent implies the Creator is more merciful.
  • The hadith of mercy. Seeing a mother clutching her baby, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah is more merciful to His servants than this woman is to her child" (Bukhari 5999).
  • Wisdom in creation. "He who perfected everything He created" (32:7); the fine-tuning, the order of nature and the suitability of the world for life all display wisdom.
  • No defects. He neither sleeps nor tires (2:255; 50:38), does not forget (20:52), does not wrong anyone (4:40), and there is nothing like Him (42:11).
Arabic

وَلِلَّهِ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ فَادْعُوهُ بِهَا

7:180

وَلِلَّهِ الْمَثَلُ الْأَعْلَىٰ ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ

16:60
Objections

"If God is merciful, why is there suffering?"

See The problem of evil and suffering: the argument there shows suffering is consistent with wisdom and mercy, especially given an afterlife of justice.

"Projecting human qualities onto God is anthropomorphism."

The principle explicitly removes the limitations that come with creaturely qualities. "Knowing" for God doesn't mean learning; "merciful" doesn't mean emotional need. The Quran affirms the attributes while denying likeness (42:11).

Practice

Your opponent says

“You've proved a powerful creator, but not a good one.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Every good in the world, including our own sense of justice and mercy, comes from the Creator, and a cause can't give what it lacks. A being who is self-sufficient has no need that could drive it to cruelty, and a wise being acts for good ends. That's why the Quran calls Him al-Rahman and al-Hakim.

In one breath

Creatures' perfections come from the Creator, who has them more fully and without defect (Ibn Taymiyya's qiyas al-awla). Hence His wisdom (32:7), mercy (Bukhari 5999) and freedom from every deficiency (42:11).

Argument 6 of 7

A wise and merciful God would guide us: from God to prophethood

Thesis

Some agnostics and deists accept a Creator but deny He communicates. But a wise Creator who made beings capable of moral choice, with a purpose, would guide them, and human reason alone cannot reach the details of that guidance. This makes revelation expected and the next question is which revelation is true.

Argument
  1. P1The Creator is wise and merciful, and made human beings with moral awareness, free choice and a desire for meaning.
  2. P2A wise maker does not create a being for a purpose and leave the purpose unknown; a merciful one does not leave his creatures without guidance on what will save or harm them eternally.
  3. P3Reason can know that God exists and is good, but cannot know on its own how He wants to be worshipped, what happens after death, or the details of His law.
  4. ∴We should expect God to communicate through messengers; the question becomes which claim to revelation is true, and the rest of this library makes the case for Islam.
Evidence
  • Purpose. "Did you think We created you without purpose and that you would not be returned to Us?" (23:115). "I did not create jinn and humankind except to worship Me" (51:56).
  • Guidance sent. "We never punish until We have sent a messenger" (17:15). "And for every nation there is a guide" (13:7).
  • Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim argue that reason and fitrah know God and the broad shape of good and evil, but the details of worship and the unseen need revelation; and that sending prophets is required by God's wisdom and mercy.
  • Deism's weakness. A deist God who creates moral beings with a longing for Him and then remains silent is less wise and less merciful than the God the arguments establish, so deism is unstable: it accepts the premises and refuses the conclusion.
Arabic

أَفَحَسِبْتُمْ أَنَّمَا خَلَقْنَاكُمْ عَبَثًا وَأَنَّكُمْ إِلَيْنَا لَا تُرْجَعُونَ

23:115
Objections

"If God wanted to communicate, He'd write it in the sky."

Then belief would be compelled and the test of free choice would disappear. God gave clear but not overwhelming signs: a preserved book, fulfilled prophecies, a messenger of evident character. These are enough for the sincere (see Proofs for Islam).

"There are many claimed revelations; how do I choose?"

By the same reasoning used here: which one is consistent with the attributes proved (one, perfect, merciful, just), and which has the strongest evidence of divine origin.

Practice

Your opponent says

“I believe in a creator, but not in religion; God doesn't need to talk to us.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

A wise and merciful Creator who made you able to choose good and evil and longing for meaning would hardly leave you without guidance. A deist God is less wise than the God your own reasoning points to. So the question isn't whether God speaks but where.

In one breath

A wise, merciful Creator who made moral beings for a purpose would guide them (23:115; 17:15). Reason knows God; revelation gives the details. Deism accepts the premises and refuses the conclusion.

Argument 7 of 7

Answering the agnostic's favourite objections

Thesis

The common reasons for agnosticism (divine hiddenness, religious diversity, "we can't know anything beyond science") either undermine themselves or are answered by the attributes already proved.

Evidence
  • "If God existed, He would make Himself obvious" (the hiddenness argument, J.L. Schellenberg). God has made Himself knowable through creation, the fitrah and revelation; what He has not done is force belief. A test of sincerity needs some freedom to turn away (67:2). And the people the argument imagines as "non-resistant non-believers" usually haven't investigated seriously.
  • "So many religions disagree, so none can be known." Disagreement doesn't mean no answer is right: people disagree about history and science too. And the major theistic religions largely agree on a Creator; the disagreement is about details that can be tested (preservation of scripture, coherence of the doctrine of God, prophecy).
  • "Only science gives knowledge" (scientism). That statement is not itself a scientific finding, so by its own standard it can't be known. Science also depends on things it cannot prove scientifically: the reliability of reason, the uniformity of nature, the reality of the external world (see Philosophy for debaters).
  • "We can't know anything about what's beyond the universe." We know some things by reasoning alone: that contradictions are impossible, that dependent things need a cause. These apply beyond the universe as much as within it. "We can't know anything about God" is itself a claim to know something about Him.
  • "I'll believe when I see a miracle." The Quran records people who said exactly that and did not believe when they saw (6:111; 54:1-2). The question is whether the available evidence is sufficient, and the earlier sections argue it is.
Arabic

الَّذِي خَلَقَ الْمَوْتَ وَالْحَيَاةَ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا

67:2
Practice

Your opponent says

“God is hidden; if He wanted me to believe, I'd believe.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

He made Himself known through creation, your own nature and a preserved revelation, but He doesn't force belief, because life is a test of what we do with the evidence (67:2). Have you examined the evidence seriously, or are you waiting to be compelled?

In one breath

Hiddenness: God is knowable but doesn't compel belief (67:2). Religious diversity doesn't mean no answer. Scientism refutes itself. "We can't know anything about God" is itself a claim of knowledge about God.
