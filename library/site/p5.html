The Quran & the earlier scriptures

7 arguments

The most popular Christian argument against Islam today is the "Islamic dilemma": the Quran confirms the Torah and Gospel; the Torah and Gospel contradict the Quran; so either the Quran confirms books that refute it, or it is wrong that they were corrupted. This section dismantles the dilemma. The Quran confirms what God revealed, not every text in circulation; it names several kinds of corruption; it calls itself the guardian and judge over earlier scripture; the early Muslims all believed the texts were altered; and the verses Christians cite (10:94, 5:47, 6:115) say less than claimed. It also answers the opposite charge, that the Quran copied the Bible and apocrypha.

Argument 1 of 7

"Confirming" means affirming what God revealed, not endorsing the texts in circulation

Thesis

The Quran confirms the revelation God gave to Moses and Jesus. It never confirms the later compilations as they stood in the 7th century, and Christians accept the same kind of selective confirmation when Jesus confirms the Torah.

Argument
  1. P1The Quran tells believers to believe in what Allah revealed to Moses and Jesus (2:136; 3:3).
  2. P2The books Jews and Christians held in the 7th century were not entirely revelation: the Gospels, for example, are accounts written about Jesus by others, not the Injil revealed to him.
  3. ∴The Quran's confirmation covers the revealed content within those books, not the books as wholes. This is a general confirmation, not a blanket one, and the dilemma's first premise is false.
Evidence
  • The idiom. Musaddiqan lima bayna yadayhi means "confirming what came before it" (Lane's Lexicon: bayna yadayhi, "before it" in time). It describes the Quran's relationship to earlier revelation, not to particular manuscripts.
  • Jesus' "confirmation". Jesus confirmed the Torah (Matt 5:17) and told people to obey the teachers on Moses' seat (Matt 23:2-3), yet he set aside the Torah's rules on oaths (Matt 5:33-37 against Num 30:2) and "eye for an eye" (Matt 5:38-39 against Exod 21:24). Christians don't say Jesus contradicted himself. Confirmation is not a blanket endorsement of every line.
  • Fulfilment. "Believe in what I have sent down, confirming what is with you" (2:41). A key sense of confirmation is fulfilment: the Quran and the Prophet ﷺ are what the earlier scriptures foretold (7:157; 61:6), the same way Christians say Jesus "confirmed" the Old Testament by fulfilling it. It also confirms the shared creed: one God, prophets, angels, judgement.
  • Naming the corrupted book. The Quran can call the book in their hands "the Torah" because that is what they claim it is, as Joseph speaks of 'many separate lords' when addressing his fellow prisoners (12:39), and because it used to be that, as "give the orphans their property" (4:2) is said of orphans who have grown up.
  • Nicolai Sinai, an Oxford scholar, explains that the Quran's term muhaymin (5:48) makes a stronger claim than "confirming": the Quran is the "ultimate arbiter" over Jewish and Christian scripture, and its confirmation involves "authoritative modification and rectification".

Objections

"The Quran says 'confirming what is with you' (2:41), meaning the texts they had."

It confirms what is with them in the sense of the prophecies and teachings they possessed. The same Quran says, in the same surah, that they "conceal the truth while you know it" (2:42), "distort it after understanding it" (2:75) and "write the book with their hands" (2:79). The Quran cannot be confirming a text it describes as altered in the same passage.

"Then the Quran should have said clearly which parts are true."

It does: it is the criterion (furqan, 25:1) and guardian (5:48). What agrees with it is confirmed; what contradicts it is rejected. That's exactly how the early commentators read it.

Practice

Your opponent says

“The Quran says it confirms the Torah and Gospel, and those contradict the Quran, so Islam refutes itself.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

The Quran confirms what God revealed to Moses and Jesus. The Gospels aren't the Injil revealed to Jesus; they're accounts written about him. Jesus "confirmed" the Torah while overriding its oath law, and you don't say he refuted himself. Same standard.

In one breath

The Quran confirms what God revealed, not every book in circulation; it calls itself muhaymin, the arbiter over earlier scripture (Sinai); and Jesus' own "confirmation" of the Torah involved changes. The dilemma's first premise is false.

Argument 2 of 7

The Quran describes the earlier scriptures as altered, and every early Muslim authority agreed

Thesis

The Quran names at least five kinds of corruption, and from the Companions onward Muslim scholars understood that the books in Jewish and Christian hands had been changed.

Argument
  1. P1If the Quran explicitly describes the People of the Book changing, concealing and writing scripture, it does not regard their texts as preserved.
  2. P2It describes concealment, twisting with the tongue, writing with their own hands, exchanging words and moving words from their places; Ibn Abbas, Ibn Kathir, Ibn Taymiyya and many others say the texts were altered.
  3. ∴The Quran and the early Muslims did not believe the Bible was preserved, so "the early Muslims believed the Bible was intact" is false.
Evidence

Six patterns of tahrif in the Quran.

  • Concealment: "you conceal the truth while you know it" (2:42; 3:71; 3:187).
  • Twisting with the tongue: "a party of them twist their tongues with the Book so you think it is from the Book, but it is not" (3:78).
  • Writing by hand: "woe to those who write the book with their own hands, then say, this is from Allah" (2:79).
  • Exchanging words: "the wrongdoers changed the word to other than what was said to them" (2:59).
  • Moving words from their places: "they distort words from their places" (4:46; 5:13).
  • And making scripture into pages, "showing some and hiding much" (6:91).

The Companions. Ibn Abbas: "How can you ask the People of the Book about anything when your Book… is the most recent? Allah has told you that the People of the Book changed and altered what Allah wrote, and wrote the book with their own hands and said 'this is from Allah'" (Bukhari 7363; 2685).

The scholars. Ibn Kathir on 3:78: the books in their hands have certainly been changed, added to and subtracted from. Ibn Taymiyya held a middle view: some wording and much meaning were altered. Al-Jahiz noted that two of the four Evangelists were not disciples and that the Gospels' contradictions show they were not protected from error. Al-Qadi Isma'il ibn Ishaq explained the difference: Allah entrusted the Torah to its people to preserve (5:44, "bima ustuhfizu", "what they were entrusted to preserve"), and they could fail, but He took the Quran's preservation on Himself (15:9). The list includes Umar, Ibn Mas'ud, Ka'b, Muqatil, Tabari, Ibn Hazm, al-Juwayni, al-Razi and al-Qurtubi. The Syriac Disputation of John and the Emir, set on 9 May 644 (preserved in an 874 manuscript; its date of composition is debated), has the emir question the integrity of the Gospel.

The "tahrif of meaning only" quote. A statement in Bukhari's chapter headings says "no one can remove a word from Allah's books, but they misinterpret them". It is a mu'allaq comment traced to Wahb ibn Munabbih or Bukhari's own gloss, not a hadith of the Prophet ﷺ, and Ibn Abbas' explicit statement in the same book contradicts it.

Objections

"Those verses are about a few Jews in Medina twisting meaning orally."

Some are. But 2:79 is about writing and attributing to God, and 6:91 about hiding parts of scripture. And Ibn Abbas explicitly says the People of the Book "changed and altered what Allah wrote".

"The Prophet put the Torah on a cushion and said 'I believe in you' (Abu Dawud 4449)."

That wording is missing from the versions in Bukhari (3635; 6841) and Muslim (1699), and its chain includes Hisham ibn Sa'd, whom Ahmad, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Hibban and Ibn Hajar weakened (al-Albani nonetheless graded it hasan). Even if accepted, the story is about the Jews of Medina hiding the verse on stoning with a hand while reading the Torah (Bukhari 3635): it shows concealment, and the Prophet ﷺ honouring what God revealed while exposing what they hid.

Ibn Abbas, a Companion, says in Bukhari 7363 that they "changed and altered what Allah wrote and wrote the book with their own hands". That is textual. The "meaning only" quote is a chapter-heading comment, not a hadith.

The Quran names concealment, tongue-twisting, writing by hand, changing words and moving words. Ibn Abbas (Bukhari 7363) says they altered what Allah wrote. Ibn Kathir, Ibn Taymiyya, al-Jahiz and al-Qadi Isma'il agree. The "meaning only" quote is not a hadith.

Argument 3 of 7

"None can change the words of Allah" is about His decree, not the Bible's text

Thesis

Verses like 6:115 and 10:64 say no one can change Allah's kalimat, meaning His decrees and promises. The Quran guarantees preservation only for itself (15:9), while the Torah was entrusted to humans (5:44).

Argument
  1. P1If "the words of Allah" in these verses meant every earlier scripture, the Quran would contradict its own statements that people altered those scriptures (2:79; 4:46).
  2. P2In context, kalimat Allah means His decree and promise: that He will help His messengers (6:34) and give glad tidings to believers (10:64).
  3. ∴These verses don't say the Bible can't be altered.
Evidence
  • 6:34: "Messengers before you were denied, and they were patient… until Our help came to them. None can alter the words of Allah." The "words" are His promise of help.
  • 10:64: "For them are glad tidings in the life of this world and the Hereafter. No change is there in the words of Allah." The promise of glad tidings.
  • 18:27: "Recite what has been revealed to you of the Book of your Lord; none can change His words." This concerns the Quran, revealed to the Prophet ﷺ.
  • Tabari and Qatada explain kalimat as His decrees and promises.
  • 15:9 promises the preservation of "the Reminder" (the Quran) by Allah Himself. 5:44 says the rabbis judged by "what they were entrusted to preserve of Allah's Book", making them the guardians, and humans can fail.
  • The word "dhikr". 16:43 tells people to "ask the people of the Reminder (ahl al-dhikr) if you do not know". There dhikr refers to the earlier revelation and its people, who can be asked about shared basics (that earlier prophets were men). In 15:9, the commentators agree, dhikr is the Quran. The meaning depends on context.

إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا الذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ

15:9

وَلَقَدْ كُذِّبَتْ رُسُلٌ مِّن قَبْلِكَ فَصَبَرُوا… حَتَّىٰ أَتَاهُمْ نَصْرُنَا ۚ وَلَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِ اللَّهِ

6:34
Objections

"If God could preserve the Quran, why not the Torah?"

He could have; He chose to entrust the Torah to the rabbis (5:44) and to preserve the final revelation Himself (15:9). Each earlier revelation was for a people and a time; the last is for all people until the end. A test given to a community to guard its book is not a failure of God.

Practice

Your opponent says

Read 6:34: the "words" there are God's promise of help to His messengers. The Quran says people did change written scripture (2:79). The only book God promised to guard Himself is the Quran (15:9); the Torah was entrusted to the rabbis (5:44).

In one breath

"None can change the words of Allah" refers to His decrees and promises (6:34; 10:64). Allah guards the Quran Himself (15:9), but entrusted the Torah to the rabbis (5:44).

Argument 4 of 7

10:94: "Ask those who read the Book before you"

Thesis

10:94 is a conditional, rhetorical statement directed through the Prophet ﷺ to doubters, about the stories of Noah and Moses just told; the people to ask are the believing People of the Book who recognised the Prophet. It does not endorse their scriptures as preserved.

Argument
  1. P1A verse must be read in its context and with the rest of the Quran's statements about the People of the Book.
  2. P210:94 follows the stories of Noah, Moses and Pharaoh (10:71-93); it is conditional ("if you are in doubt") and ends "so never be among the doubters"; the commentators say the Prophet neither doubted nor asked; the Quran elsewhere calls the People of the Book unreliable witnesses and praises those of them who believed.
  3. ∴10:94 refers to confirmation of the prophetic stories by believing scholars like Abdullah ibn Salam, not to an endorsement of the Bible.
Evidence
  • Context. The preceding verses tell how Noah was rejected and saved, and how Moses and Aaron confronted Pharaoh, who drowned. These are in Genesis 6-9 and Exodus 2-14, and anyone who read the Torah could confirm them.
  • Rhetorical. "If you are in doubt" does not mean the Prophet doubted. Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "He neither doubted nor asked" (Tabari). The grammarians Tha'lab and al-Mubarrad, and al-Qurtubi, explain that the address is to the doubting listener through the Prophet.
  • Who to ask. Ibn Abbas and Tabari: those of the People of the Book who knew Muhammad ﷺ from their books and believed, such as Abdullah ibn Salam (46:10; 13:43; 28:52-53). Ibn Kathir links it to their recognising him "as they recognise their own sons" (2:146).
  • The Prophet's own instruction. "Do not believe the People of the Book and do not deny them; say: we believe in Allah and what was revealed" (Bukhari 4485). That makes no sense if 10:94 told him to treat their books as authoritative.
  • Ibn Taymiyya (al-Jawab al-Sahih) lists what the earlier books still testify to: the monotheism of the prophets, that messengers were human, how nations treated their prophets, the core of religion, and the signs of Muhammad ﷺ.
Arabic

فَإِن كُنتَ فِي شَكٍّ مِّمَّا أَنزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ فَاسْأَلِ الَّذِينَ يَقْرَءُونَ الْكِتَابَ مِن قَبْلِكَ ۚ لَقَدْ جَاءَكَ الْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّكَ فَلَا تَكُونَنَّ مِنَ الْمُمْتَرِينَ

10:94
Objections

"'Those who read the Book' means all Jews and Christians, so their books are reliable."

Reliable enough to confirm the story of Noah's flood or the Exodus, which are still there. The verse doesn't say their books are free of change, and the same Quran says they were changed.

Practice

Your opponent says

“10:94 tells Muhammad to check his revelation against the Bible, so the Bible must be the standard.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

The verse ends "the truth has come to you from your Lord, so never be among the doubters". The Quran is the standard (5:48). The People of the Book can confirm shared stories, and the believers among them (like Abdullah ibn Salam) confirmed that the Prophet was the one their books foretold.

In one breath

10:94 comes right after the stories of Noah and Moses, is conditional and rhetorical, and points to believing scholars like Abdullah ibn Salam. The Prophet ﷺ told Muslims neither to believe nor deny the People of the Book.

Argument 5 of 7

"Let the people of the Gospel judge by what Allah revealed in it" (5:47)

Thesis

5:47 tells Christians to judge by what Allah revealed in the Gospel, not by every text in their books, and the next verse makes the Quran the guardian. Islamic law itself overrides their books, which shows the Quran never treated them as fully binding.

Argument
  1. P1If the Quran held the Torah and Gospel to be fully binding, it would not abrogate their laws or require Jews and Christians to accept the Prophet's prohibitions.
  2. P25:47 says "by what Allah revealed in it"; 5:48 makes the Quran guardian; 9:29 faults the People of the Book for not forbidding what Allah and His Messenger forbade; and 6:146 explains that Jewish dietary restrictions were a penalty.
  3. ∴5:47 refers to the revealed core of the Gospel (including its prophecy of Muhammad ﷺ), judged by the Quran.
Evidence
  • The wording. "Let the people of the Gospel judge by what Allah revealed in it" (5:47). It is not "judge by it". The commentator Muqatil explains this as the true commands within it, such as the command to pardon a killer.
  • The next verse (5:48) makes the Quran muhaymin over the earlier books and tells the Prophet to judge between them by what Allah revealed to him.
  • 5:66 and 5:68 ("if they had upheld the Torah and the Gospel and what was revealed to them from their Lord") include "what was revealed to them from their Lord", which the commentators take to include the Quran.
  • 9:29 criticises the People of the Book who "do not forbid what Allah and His Messenger have forbidden". If the Torah and Gospel were sufficient, the Prophet's prohibitions would not bind them.
  • 6:146 says the Jewish dietary restrictions were imposed "for their transgression", and the Prophet ﷺ said that if Moses were alive he would have to follow him (Ahmad and al-Darimi, from Jabir; the chain is disputed and some graders accept it as hasan through supporting reports).
Objections

"5:47 shows the Gospel existed in the Prophet's time."

Some form of the Christian Gospel existed (possibly the Diatessaron among Arabic-speaking Christians; see next argument). The verse tells its people to act on what God revealed in it, chief of which is the prophecy of the coming messenger (7:157; 61:6).

Practice

Your opponent says

“The Quran tells Christians to judge by the Gospel, so it must be reliable.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

It says judge by "what Allah revealed in it", and the next verse makes the Quran the guardian over it. And the Quran overrides it: 9:29 binds them to what Allah and His Messenger forbade. A book that is overridden is not treated as fully authoritative.

In one breath

5:47 says judge by "what Allah revealed in it"; 5:48 makes the Quran the guardian; 9:29 binds the People of the Book to the Prophet's prohibitions; and Moses himself would have had to follow Muhammad ﷺ. The Quran never treats their books as fully binding.

Argument 6 of 7

The Quran's Torah and Injil are revelations, not the Bible's books, and there was no Arabic Bible

Thesis

In the Quran, the Torah is what was given to Moses and the Injil is what was given to Jesus. Neither is the Pentateuch or the four Gospels. And there was no Arabic Bible in the Prophet's time from which he could have learned or copied.

Argument
  1. P1The Quran's terms must be defined by the Quran's usage.
  2. P2The Quran says Moses was given the Tablets and the Torah, and Jesus was taught the Injil by God (3:48; 5:46). The New Testament Gospels are, by Christian admission, accounts written by others about Jesus.
  3. P3The earliest Arabic translations of the Bible date from the 8th-9th centuries.
  4. ∴The Quran's "Injil" is not the New Testament, and the Prophet ﷺ had no access to a written Arabic Bible.
Evidence
  • The Injil was given to Jesus. "He will teach him the Book and wisdom, the Torah and the Injil" (3:48); "We gave him the Injil, in which was guidance and light" (5:46). The four Gospels are "according to" Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, written decades after Jesus.
  • The Torah. The Quran speaks of the Tablets given to Moses containing "instruction concerning all things" (7:145). It never says Moses wrote five books including his own death (Deut 34).
  • No Arabic Bible. Bruce Metzger: the earliest Arabic Bible translations probably date from the 8th century. Among the earliest Arabic Gospel manuscripts are Vatican Arabic 13 (undated, 9th century) and Sinai Arabic 72 (dated 897). Sidney Griffith (The Bible in Arabic) agrees, and notes that the Gospel the Quran confirms is not the Gospel as Christians had it.
  • No Christian community in the Hijaz. Stephen Shoemaker (Creating the Qur'an) notes that the Hijaz shows little or no evidence of an organised Christian community (citing Beaucamp, Robin, Trimingham). Arthur Vööbus called the idea that Muhammad used an Arabic New Testament unsubstantiated.
  • The Diatessaron. The "Gospel" known to Arabic-speaking Christians, like Waraqa ibn Nawfal, may have been Tatian's Diatessaron (c. 170), a single harmonised Gospel that dominated Syriac churches until the 5th century, though this is a hypothesis. The Arabic Diatessaron preserves early Syriac readings.

Argument 7 of 7

The Quran did not copy the apocrypha

Thesis

The Quran shares some stories with non-canonical texts (Jesus' clay birds, "what no eye has seen", Moses in Midian), but the parallels are with older, shared traditions, the Quran's versions differ in theologically significant ways, and the supposed sources are often later than the Quran.

Argument
  1. P1Copying requires access to the source, and the copy should reproduce the source's distinctive features.
  2. P2The Quran's versions lack the distinctive features of the alleged sources (and sometimes correct them), the sources were not available in Arabic, and some are later than the Quran.
  3. ∴The parallels reflect shared earlier tradition, not copying.
Evidence

The clay birds (3:49; 5:110) and the Infancy Gospel of Thomas. In the Infancy Gospel, a five-year-old Jesus makes twelve sparrows on the Sabbath, quarrels about the Sabbath, and elsewhere kills a teacher and a child with curses and is worshipped. In the Quran, Jesus as a prophet makes "the form of a bird", and it becomes a bird "by Allah's permission" (bi-idhni-llah), repeated pointedly. The Quran takes nothing of the Infancy Gospel's distinctive content and adds what it lacks: dependence on God. The Infancy Gospel's oldest fragment is a 4th/5th-century papyrus, and complete manuscripts of the story are medieval. Scholars like Fred Lapham think some stories go back to early oral tradition, and Irenaeus (c. 180) knew one. John 21:25 itself says much of what Jesus did was never written. Even the critic Sean Anthony concedes the Quran did not copy the Infancy Gospel.

"What no eye has seen" (hadith qudsi, Bukhari 3244; 1 Cor 2:9). Paul introduces it with "as it is written", so he is quoting an older source. The same saying appears, attributed to Jesus, in the Gospel of Thomas (17), in 1 Clement (34:8), the Gospel of Judas and other texts. Origen thought Paul was citing an apocryphon (Clivaz and Schulthess). A shared earlier prophetic saying is not copying from Paul.

Moses in Midian (28:22-28) "confused with Jacob". The "Jacob" details critics cite (a rock on the well, seven years' service, a daughter named Leah) are in later commentaries and Isra'iliyyat, not the Quran (Gabriel Reynolds, The Qur'an and Its Biblical Subtext). Meeting a wife at a well is a common ancient motif (Gen 24 with Rebekah), and working in place of a bride-price was a known custom (Kitchen). The proposed rabbinic source, Exodus Rabbah 1:32, dates to the 10th century or later. The Quran's eight to ten years are not Jacob's seven.

Manichaeans. The Encyclopaedia of Islam describes Central Arabia as "hermetically sealed" against Byzantine and Persian missions, and François de Blois finds no evidence the Meccans knew the southern Babylonian sects.

Objections

"Even if not copied directly, Muhammad heard these stories orally."

Then the source was oral tradition circulating among Christians, which may preserve true events about Jesus that the canonical Gospels did not record (John 21:25). The Quran presents its account as revelation that corrects the theology around such stories.

Practice

Your opponent says

“The Quran copies a 2nd-century fairy tale about clay birds.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

The Quran's account shares only the miracle itself, and puts it "by Allah's permission", while dropping the Infancy Gospel's child Jesus who kills people. Irenaeus already knew stories from this tradition in 180, so it may preserve an early memory. A shared event isn't a copy.

In one breath

The clay-bird story in the Quran lacks the Infancy Gospel's distinctive content and adds "by Allah's permission"; "what no eye has seen" is an older saying Paul himself quotes; and the "Jacob" details in Moses' Midian story come from later tafsir, not the Quran.

Argument 1 of 9

"The Jews say Ezra is the son of God" (9:30)

Thesis

9:30 reports what a group of Jews said, not the official creed of all Judaism. Jewish texts of the period exalted Ezra to a near-heavenly status, the Jews of Medina never denied the verse, and the Quran's style uses a general term for a particular group.

Argument
  1. P1The Quran reports sayings "with their mouths", not formal creeds.
  2. P2Jewish literature around the time exalted Ezra as the restorer of the Torah who was taken up to live with God's "son"; the scribal figure (Ezra/Enoch) was elevated to angelic status; and the Jews of Medina, who were hostile to the Prophet, never objected that no Jew said this.
  3. ∴9:30 accurately reports a belief held by some Jews, and is not an error.
Evidence
  • "With their mouths." The verse continues: "that is their saying with their mouths; they imitate the saying of those who disbelieved before" (9:30). It describes speech, not scripture.
  • Ezra exalted. In 4 Ezra (late 1st century CE), Ezra restores the lost scriptures by divine inspiration and is told: "you shall be taken up from among men, and henceforth you shall live with My Son (the Messiah in 4 Ezra's usage)" (4 Ezra 14:9). Ezra 7:6 already describes him as a scribe on whom "the hand of the LORD his God" rested and who was granted all he asked. Jewish tradition fused the figure of the inspired Scribe (Ezra/Enoch) with angelic status; in 3 Enoch, Enoch becomes Metatron, called "the lesser YHWH" (3 Enoch 12:5). Angels were called "sons of God" in Jewish texts (Gen 6:2; Job 1:6).
  • Scholars. Gordon Newby (A History of the Jews of Arabia) argues Ezra could be counted among the bene elohim in Arabian Jewish circles; Reuven Firestone finds it plausible that a Jewish group elevated Ezra to near-divine status.
  • Medina's Jews didn't object. Ibn Abbas reports that Jewish leaders of Medina (Sallam ibn Mishkam and others) told the Prophet ﷺ they could not follow him because he did not hold Uzayr to be the son of Allah. 'Ubayd ibn 'Umayr says the saying came from Finhas ibn 'Azura. Ibn al-'Arabi notes that the Jews of Medina never objected to the verse, which they would have if it were baseless.
  • General word, specific meaning. Al-Qurtubi explains that Arabic can use a general term for part of a group (itlaq ism al-kull 'ala al-ba'd), as in 3:173, "the people said to them, the people have gathered against you", where "the people" was a few men. The Bible does the same: "the world cannot receive him" (John 14:17).

Argument 2 of 9

Mary and the Trinity (5:116; 5:73)

Thesis

The Quran does not define the official Trinity as Father, Son and Mary. It condemns every form of "God is one of three", and it accurately condemns the deification of Mary that was real among Christians of its time.

Argument
  1. P1The Quran addresses what Christians of its era actually believed and practised, not only the creeds of councils.
  2. P25:73 rejects saying "Allah is the third of three"; 5:116 asks whether Jesus told people to take him and his mother as gods, which condemns the worship of Mary; and there is ample evidence of Mary being treated as divine.
  3. ∴The Quran's statements are accurate descriptions of real Christian belief and practice.
Evidence
  • What the verses say. 5:73: "They have disbelieved who say Allah is the third of three." 5:116: "Did you say to people, take me and my mother as gods besides Allah?" Neither says that the official Trinity consists of Father, Son and Mary. "Third of three" matches the Christian formula that the Son is "one of the Trinity" (unus ex Trinitate), promoted under Justinian. 5:72's "Allah is the Messiah" echoes the Syriac theologian Henana, criticised by Babai the Great for saying "Christ is God and God is Christ".
  • Mary worshipped. Epiphanius describes the Collyridians, women who offered cakes to Mary as to a goddess (Panarion 79). The Christian historian Eutychius (Sa'id ibn Batriq, 9th-10th century) mentions "Mariamites" who held Jesus and his mother to be two gods besides God.
  • Mary as divine protector. In the 620s, as the Quran was being revealed, Constantinople credited Mary with saving the city from the Avar siege of 626, when Patriarch Sergius carried her icon on the walls (Heraclius had sailed with a Marian icon in 610). She was hailed as the city's invincible protector (the Akathistos tradition, traditionally linked to 626). The Quran answers: "who could do anything against Allah if He wished to destroy the Messiah, son of Mary, and his mother?" (5:17); "they both used to eat food" (5:75).
  • Later Marian devotion. Alphonsus Liguori's The Glories of Mary quotes Bernardine of Siena "at the command of Mary all obey, even God". Popes still entrust the world and people's salvation to her. Calvin mocked churches displaying vials of Mary's milk.

Argument 5 of 9

The story of the cow (2:67-74)

Thesis

Critics say the Quran confused the red heifer (Numbers 19) with the heifer of an unsolved murder (Deuteronomy 21). In fact it deliberately combines and reverses them to make a point about Israel's hardness of heart, and its sequence follows the Torah's own order.

Argument
  1. P1A conscious reworking of two texts that reverses their points and follows their sequence is not confusion.
  2. P2The Quran takes the cow's description from Numbers 19 and the murder from Deuteronomy 21, but turns the unsolved murder into a concealed one that the cow exposes, and makes the community guilty rather than innocent. Only the closing image (2:74, stones from which rivers gush) echoes the rock of Numbers 20; the rock episode itself is at 2:60.
  3. ∴The Quran's story is a deliberate, informed retelling.
Evidence
  • Numbers 19 describes a red heifer, without blemish, "on which a yoke has never come". The Quran's cow is a specific colour, flawless, "not trained to plough the earth or water the fields" (2:69-71).
  • Deuteronomy 21:1-9 prescribes a heifer when a murdered man is found and the killer is unknown; the elders declare "our hands did not shed this blood". In the Quran, the community knows more than it admits: "you disputed over it, but Allah was to bring out what you were concealing" (2:72), and the cow's part restores the dead man to name his killer (2:73).
  • The sequence. Numbers 19 (red heifer) is followed by Numbers 20 (water from the rock). The Quran follows the cow with: "then your hearts became hardened like stones, or even harder; for there are stones from which rivers gush forth" (2:74), echoing both the rock and Ezekiel's "heart of stone" (Ezek 36:26).
  • Rabbinic links. Rabbinic tradition connects the red heifer with atonement for the golden calf, which the Quran mentions just before (2:51-54).
  • No ashes. The Quran omits the ashes used for purification, because in its story the cow serves a single miraculous purpose.
Objections

"Mixing two laws is still a mistake."

The Quran isn't restating Torah law; it's narrating an episode in which Israel's stalling and concealment are exposed, and it draws on two Torah passages to do it, just as the NT writers merge OT texts (Mark 1:2-3 merges three).

Practice

It uses both on purpose and reverses their points: the murder is concealed, not unknown, and the guilty community is exposed instead of cleared. It then follows Numbers' own sequence from the heifer to water from the rock (2:74). That is skilful allusion, not confusion.

Argument 6 of 9

Inheritance: the shares and the fractions (4:11-12)

Thesis

The inheritance verses are not mathematically flawed: Islamic law has always handled cases where shares exceed or fall short of the estate ('awl and radd). And the woman's "half share" applies only in some cases, tied to financial duties that fall on men.

Argument
  1. P1A legal system that provides fixed shares and a rule for scaling them when they exceed the whole is complete, not erroneous.
  2. P2Islamic law scales shares proportionally ('awl) when they exceed one, and redistributes any remainder (radd) when they fall short. Women's shares depend on kinship, generation and financial obligation, and in many cases equal or exceed men's.
  3. ∴The inheritance law is coherent and just.
Evidence
  • 'Awl. When fixed shares add up to more than the estate (for example a wife 1/8, two daughters 2/3, both parents 1/6 each), all shares are reduced proportionally. The Caliph Umar introduced this, and the Sunni schools later adopted it (Ibn Abbas dissented). Any modern law that guarantees several fixed claims must scale them when assets fall short, as in bankruptcy.
  • Radd. When shares add up to less, most schools (Hanafi, Hanbali, later Shafi'i) return the remainder to the share-holders other than spouses; Malikis and early Shafi'is send it to the public treasury.
  • Why some men get double. Shares are set by (1) closeness of kinship, (2) generation (the younger generation, who have life ahead, often inherit more than the older), and (3) financial duty. Only in the third category does a son receive twice a daughter's share, because he must pay the dowry (mahr) and maintain his wife, children and often his parents and sisters, while a woman's wealth is entirely her own and she has no duty to spend it on the family.
  • Women often equal or more. Uterine brothers and sisters inherit equally (4:12). A father and mother each get a sixth when there are children (4:11). In some cases a daughter receives more than a male relative, such as the deceased's brother.
Arabic

يُوصِيكُمُ اللَّهُ فِي أَوْلَادِكُمْ ۖ لِلذَّكَرِ مِثْلُ حَظِّ الْأُنثَيَيْنِ

"If God wrote the shares, why would they ever add up to more than one?"

It is unequal in amount but matched by unequal duty. A brother who inherits twice as much must spend it on his family; his sister keeps hers. Equality of outcome under unequal obligations would be unjust.

Argument 7 of 9

  • The poisoning made him a martyr, as Ibn Mas'ud and others said, and he was not "seized by the right hand" in the act of fabricating.

"He used the same word, watin, so he admitted the verse came true."

He described a feeling ("as if"), not an event. And the verse's punishment is immediate seizure while fabricating, not a slow death years later after his mission was complete.

Argument 8 of 9

The war verses do not cancel the peace verses

Thesis

The claim that 9:5 abrogated all peace verses has no basis in the Quran or Sunnah, according to major commentators. Most classical jurists (Hanafi, Maliki, Hanbali) held that the cause of fighting is being a combatant or belonging to a hostile polity, not disbelief itself; Shafi'is disagreed.

Argument
  1. P1An abrogation claim needs evidence from the Quran or Sunnah.
  2. P2Tabari, Ibn Kathir and al-Zarkashi reject the claim that 8:61 ("if they incline to peace, incline to it") was abrogated; the peace verses are Medinan, revealed alongside the war verses; and the majority of jurists made aggression, not disbelief, the cause of fighting.
  3. ∴Islam's peace verses remain in force, and the war verses are conditional on aggression or treaty-breaking.
Evidence
  • 8:61 "If they incline to peace, then incline to it." Tabari rejects the view that it was abrogated by 9:5, saying there is no evidence for it in the Book or the Sunnah. Ibn Kathir and al-Zarkashi (al-Burhan 1/48) say the same.

Objections about the Prophet ﷺ and his religion

7 arguments

These objections target the Prophet ﷺ himself and the origins of Islam: that his revelations were epilepsy or demonic, that he worshipped idols, that Allah was the moon god or Hubal, that the Kaaba and Hajj are pagan, that he recited "Satanic verses", that his marriage to Zaynab was scandalous, and that Islam is racist. Each answer uses the strongest historical sources and, where relevant, applies the same standard to the Bible's prophets.

Argument 1 of 7

The experience of revelation was not epilepsy or possession

Thesis

The physical signs that accompanied revelation (sweating, heaviness, the sound like a bell) do not match epilepsy, and they parallel what the Bible says happened to prophets like Daniel. The Prophet's ﷺ faculties were intact to the end, and Satan does not preach against Satan.

Argument
  1. P1Epilepsy and possession produce confusion, loss of awareness and, over time, decline; they do not produce a coherent book recited back exactly.
  2. P2During revelation the Prophet ﷺ remained aware, then recited the revealed text exactly; he showed no decline over 23 years; and biblical prophets had similar overwhelming experiences.
  3. ∴The revelation experience was not epilepsy or possession.
Evidence
  • What happened. Aisha: "I saw him receiving revelation on a very cold day, and when it ended his forehead was dripping with sweat" (Bukhari 2). His face reddened and he breathed heavily (Bukhari 4985). The Quran calls it "a weighty word" (73:5). He said it sometimes came "like the ringing of a bell" (Bukhari 2), a simile for its intensity, and sometimes the angel came in the form of a man, which was easier.
  • Not epilepsy. W. Montgomery Watt (Muhammad at Mecca, p. 57) rejects the epilepsy theory: epilepsy causes degeneration, while Muhammad kept full mental vigour to the end; such physical accompaniments neither prove nor disprove revelation.
  • Ibn Khaldun explains the strain as the prophetic soul being lifted momentarily from human to angelic perception.
  • Biblical parallels. Daniel, seeing the angel: "no strength was left in me… I fell into a deep sleep on my face… I have no strength, and no breath is left in me" (Dan 10:8-17). Jacob was wrestled and his hip dislocated (Gen 32:24-25); Zechariah was terrified and struck mute (Luke 1:12, 20).
  • The first revelation. Gabriel pressed him at Hira (Bukhari 3), as angels overwhelmed Daniel. Later reports say Gabriel reassured him: "O Muhammad, you are the Messenger of Allah" (al-Bidaya 3/21), and further comfort followed (Surah al-Duha; Bukhari 1125).
  • Satan's work? Abu Lahab's wife taunted that his "devil" had left him. Jesus too was accused of Beelzebub (Mark 3:22), and answered that Satan does not cast out Satan (see Dilemmas for Christians).
Objections

"Even if not epilepsy, it could be a psychological condition."

A condition that produced, over 23 years, a text that its hostile audience couldn't match, a coherent law and a successful community, while its subject remained lucid and was trusted by everyone as al-Amin, is not like any known disorder.

Argument 2 of 7

Hubal was a specific imported idol whom the Prophet ﷺ opposed by name; the moon-god theory has no evidence; and Allah is the name Arab Christians and Jews used for the God of Abraham.

  1. P1If Allah were Hubal, the Prophet ﷺ would not have contrasted them.
  2. P2At Uhud, Abu Sufyan shouted "Exalted be Hubal!" and the Prophet ﷺ told the Muslims to answer "Allah is Higher and more Majestic" (Bukhari 3039); Hubal was a red agate statue brought from outside Arabia; and the moon-god theory rests on no evidence.

"The pagan Meccans worshipped 'Allah' too, so Islam continued paganism."

Objections to the Hadith

5 arguments

Critics often quote a hadith out of context, from a weak chain, or from a report that is not even attributed to the Prophet ﷺ. The first answer is always methodological: is it authentic, is it from the Prophet, and what does it actually say? Muslim scholarship has graded hadith rigorously for over a thousand years, including criticising narrations inside Sahih Muslim. This section first shows that the hadith sciences are self-critical, then answers the most common objections one by one.

Argument 1 of 5

Hadith criticism is rigorous, even about the Sahih collections

Thesis

Muslim scholars criticised and rejected weak reports, including a narration in Sahih Muslim, by examining both chains and content. That honesty is why a critic cannot treat every report as "what Islam teaches".

Argument
  1. P1A tradition that tests its own sources against strict criteria, even when the result is uncomfortable, is trustworthy in its accepted core.
  2. P2Hadith scholars rejected reports with broken chains, unknown or confused narrators, and content that contradicts the Quran, and they criticised specific narrations in the Sahih collections themselves.
  3. ∴Islamic teaching is defined by authenticated reports, not by every report a critic can find.
Evidence
  • Muslim 2789 ("Allah created the soil on Saturday…", seven days of creation). Bukhari, in al-Tarikh al-Kabir, identified it as a saying of Ka'b al-Ahbar (a convert from Judaism), not the Prophet ﷺ. Ibn Taymiyya (Majmu' 17/235) and 'Ali al-Qari agreed. It contradicts the Quran's six days. Scholars flagged a defect in a narration inside Sahih Muslim, the second most respected collection.
  • The methods. Narrators were judged on memory and honesty; chains were checked for breaks; a narrator's memory decline (ikhtilat) dates were recorded so that late hearing could be rejected; and content (matn) was checked against the Quran and established Sunnah.
  • Western scholarship. Harald Motzki's study of the Musannaf of 'Abd al-Razzaq argued that much of the material can be traced to the late first or early second Islamic century (see Preservation of the Hadith).
Objections

"Muslims just call any hadith they don't like weak."

The gradings are centuries old, made by scholars who had no stake in modern debates, using fixed criteria that apply equally to reports Muslims like.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Your own Sahih Muslim says creation took seven days, contradicting the Quran.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Bukhari himself identified that narration as Ka'b al-Ahbar's saying, not the Prophet's, and Ibn Taymiyya agreed. Muslim scholars caught the error inside their own most respected books, which shows the system works.

In one breath

Bukhari traced Muslim 2789 to Ka'b al-Ahbar, not the Prophet; Ibn Taymiyya agreed. Hadith critics examined chains, memory decline and content, even in the Sahihs, so critics can't treat every report as Islam.

Thesis

  1. P1The number of reports a Companion narrated depends on how long he lived after the Prophet, how much he devoted himself to learning, and how much he taught.
  2. P2Abu Hurayra lived with the Prophet ﷺ full-time, had his memory blessed by the Prophet's prayer, and taught for decades in Medina; other junior Companions like Ibn Umar and Ibn Abbas also narrated thousands.
  3. ∴His number of narrations is not suspicious.
Evidence

  • Others. Ibn Umar has about 2,600 narrations and Ibn Abbas, who was only nine to fourteen when the Prophet died, about 1,700. Jonathan Brown (Hadith, pp. 18-19) notes that junior Companions who lived long and taught widely narrated the most.
  • Counting. The 5,300 figure counts every chain separately; the number of distinct reports is far smaller.

Argument 5 of 5

Other hadith objections answered

Thesis

Most remaining objections come from weak reports, reports not from the Prophet ﷺ, or misreadings.

Evidence

The principle in the hadith is that water stays pure unless its smell, colour or taste changes. Al-Khattabi explains that the Companions never threw filth in; rain washed refuse into the well from the surrounding ground. Islam forbids urinating in standing water.
  • "The Black Stone expiates sins" (Tirmidhi). The Black Stone report (Tirmidhi 959) is graded hasan by Tirmidhi and sahih by al-Albani, so the answer is to explain it as expiation through obedience, not worship of the stone.

Ibn Abbas condemned it, quoting the Prophet's ﷺ ban: "Do not punish with Allah's punishment [fire]" (Bukhari 3017). At most it was 'Ali's error, corrected by a Companion with the Prophet's ruling.
  • "Allah will give each Muslim a Jew or Christian as ransom from Hell" (Muslim 2767). Every person has a place in Paradise and a place in Hell; the believer's place in Hell goes to the disbeliever who earned it. Al-Nawawi explains it as ransom (fida'), not transferring sins.
  • "If you did not sin, Allah would replace you with people who sin and seek forgiveness" (Muslim 2749). It is about Allah's love of repentance, not encouraging sin. Compare Luke 15:7: "there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons."
  • Moses' stone running off with his clothes; stones speaking. The Bible has stones that "cry out" (Luke 19:40; Hab 2:11) and a stone that "has heard all the words of the LORD" (Josh 24:27).
  • "Monkeys stoning an adulterous monkey" (Bukhari 3849). It is the observation of 'Amr ibn Maymun, a Successor, before he became Muslim, not a saying of the Prophet ﷺ; Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says the longer version has weak chains.
  • "Father, forgive them, for they know not" (Bukhari 3477). The Prophet ﷺ narrated a prophet saying this while wiping blood from his face, so it is not about a crucifixion. Qadi 'Iyad says it describes the Prophet himself at Uhud; 'Ubayd ibn 'Umayr attributes the words to Noah. Luke 23:34, where Jesus says it, is missing from P75, the first hand of Sinaiticus, Vaticanus and Bezae.

Morality in Islam

8 arguments

Most attacks on Islam today are moral rather than theological: its treatment of women, slavery, war and non-Muslims. This section answers them in three steps. First, read the rule in its full legal context, including the conditions and limits the scholars attached. Second, check whether the report behind the charge is authentic. Third, compare with what the critic's own tradition taught at the time and what Islam replaced. On each point, Islamic law turns out to be a reform that limited harm and protected the weak.

Argument 1 of 8

Women's intellect, testimony and status

Thesis

The hadith about women's "deficiency in intellect and religion" refers to reduced legal burdens, was said lightly in praise of women's persuasive power, and Islamic law weighs testimony by reliability, not sex. Women were among Islam's leading scholars.

Argument
  1. P1A text must be read with its wording, tone and the law it refers to.
  2. P2The hadith (Bukhari 304; 1462) explains the "deficiency" as the two-woman rule in commercial contracts and the exemption from prayer and fasting during menstruation, both lighter burdens, and it begins with praise of women's ability to sway "a resolute man's" mind.
  3. ∴The hadith does not teach that women are inferior in intelligence or worth.
Evidence
  • The text. The Prophet ﷺ told a group of women: "I have not seen anyone deficient in intellect and religion more able to take away the reason of a resolute man than one of you." When a woman asked what the deficiency was, he explained: the testimony of two women in place of one man (2:282), and not praying or fasting during menstruation. Both are legal concessions. The tone is gentle teasing, and one of the women who questioned him is described as "highly intelligent" (jazla, Muslim 79).
  • "Naqs" means reduction. The Quran uses the same root for "reducing the land from its borders" (13:41); it means a smaller share, not a defect.
  • The two-woman rule is limited to commercial contracts, and the verse gives the reason: "so that if one errs, the other can remind her" (2:282), reflecting women's limited involvement in trade at the time. Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim held that the purpose is reliability, so a more credible woman's testimony can outweigh a man's, and in many matters a woman's testimony alone is accepted.
  • Women scholars. The Companions brought their hardest questions to Aisha (Tirmidhi 3883). Al-Dhahabi lists over a hundred women hadith narrators, and he said no woman was ever accused of lying in hadith.
  • Christian comparison. Paul: "women should keep silent in the churches… if they want to learn anything, let them ask their husbands at home" (1 Cor 14:34-35). Tertullian called woman "the devil's gateway".

فَإِن لَّمْ يَكُونَا رَجُلَيْنِ فَرَجُلٌ وَامْرَأَتَانِ مِمَّن تَرْضَوْنَ مِنَ الشُّهَدَاءِ أَن تَضِلَّ إِحْدَاهُمَا فَتُذَكِّرَ إِحْدَاهُمَا الْأُخْرَىٰ

Argument 2 of 8

4:34 and domestic violence

Thesis

4:34 allows, as a last resort after admonition and separation, a light, non-injurious strike (ghayr mubarrih) that may not injure, break skin or leave a mark. The Prophet ﷺ never struck a woman, condemned men who did, and the jurists allowed a wife to divorce and claim redress if harmed. The hadith "a man will not be asked why he beat his wife" is weak.

Argument
  1. P1The meaning of a legal verse is defined by the Prophet's ﷺ explanation, his practice and the jurists' conditions.
  2. P2The Prophet ﷺ restricted the strike to one that leaves no mark, called men who strike their wives "not the best of you", never struck anyone himself, and allowed a struck wife to divorce. The jurists made it a last resort, conditioned on benefit and harmlessness, with a judge to punish abusers.
  3. ∴Islam does not permit domestic abuse; it restricts an existing practice almost to nothing.
Evidence
  • The Prophet's practice. "The Messenger of Allah never struck anything with his hand, neither a woman nor a servant" (Muslim 2328).
  • His condemnation. When many women came complaining that their husbands beat them, he said: "These are not the best of you" (Abu Dawud 2146). In the Farewell Sermon: "Treat women well; they are your partners."
  • A wife's redress. When Thabit ibn Qays struck his wife Habiba bint Sahl, the Prophet ﷺ allowed her to leave the marriage (Abu Dawud 2228).
  • The limit. Ibn Abbas explained the strike as with a siwak (a small tooth-stick) or similar; al-Hasan al-Basri said "not leaving a mark". Al-Shafi'i: leaving it is better (al-Umm). Al-Buhuti: at most a rolled handkerchief, never a whip or stick.
  • The jurists' protections. If a husband harms her, she can seek divorce and retribution (al-Dardir, al-Sharh al-Kabir); the judge punishes the abusive husband (al-Kasani, Bada'i'); Ibn 'Abidin: he may not bruise or break skin; Ibn Qudama provides arbitration (4:35).
  • The weak hadith. "A man will not be asked why he beat his wife" (Abu Dawud 2147; Ibn Majah 1986) comes through the unknown narrator 'Abd al-Rahman al-Musli; al-Albani and al-Dhahabi grade it weak.
  • Christian comparison. Basil of Caesarea advised a wife to endure her husband's abuse rather than separate.

The verse addressed a society where husbands beat wives freely; it restricted that to a last-resort symbolic gesture that the Prophet ﷺ himself never used and scholars discouraged. Abuse, causing pain or injury, is forbidden and punishable in Islamic law.

"The Prophet ﷺ struck Aisha" (Muslim 974, lahadani).

Lahada means a push or nudge on the chest (Lisan al-'Arab; al-Nawawi), a gesture he used with Ubayy ibn Ka'b and others to dispel doubt or anxiety. It was a push on the chest, not a blow with the hand, though Aisha says it hurt her. She also reported that he never struck a woman (Muslim 2328).

"Tabari said a husband can tie his wife up."

Tabari offered that reading of hajr (separation), and later scholars rejected it: al-Zamakhshari called it "the interpretation of the ignorant", and al-Mawardi and Ibn 'Atiyya rejected it too.

Argument 3 of 8

Polygyny: limited, conditioned and discouraged

Thesis

Islam took unlimited polygamy and limited it to four, conditioned on justice, and the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools recommended keeping to one. A wife may stipulate in her contract that her husband take no second wife.

Argument
  1. P1A law should be judged by what it replaced and the conditions it imposed.
  2. P2Pre-Islamic Arabs had unlimited wives; the Quran limited it to four and said "if you fear you will not be just, then one" (4:3), adding "you will never be able to be perfectly just" (4:129).

  • The condition. "Marry those that please you, two, three or four; but if you fear you will not be just, then one" (4:3). "A man who has two wives and favours one will come on the Day of Resurrection with one side leaning" (Abu Dawud 2133).
  • Recommended one. Al-Shafi'i, Ibn Qudama, al-Buhuti, al-Nawawi and al-Mawardi recommend keeping to one wife.
  • The wife's right. Imam Ahmad's school allows a wife to stipulate in the marriage contract that her husband will take no other wife, and the stipulation is binding.
  • The Prophet's marriages were mostly to widows and for social or political reasons: his marriage to Juwayriya led the Companions to free a hundred families of her tribe.
  • The Bible has no limit: Solomon had 700 wives (1 Kgs 11:3), and God "gave" David Saul's wives (2 Sam 12:8). See Morality in the Bible & the Church.

Argument 4 of 8

Slavery: regulated toward abolition

Thesis

Islam inherited a world in which slavery was universal. It closed most routes into slavery, opened many routes out, made freeing slaves an act of worship and expiation, punished abuse by freeing the slave, and forbade forcing slave women into prostitution (24:33), with some jurists objecting to compelled intercourse.

Argument
  1. P1A law in a world where slavery was universal should be judged by whether it reduced and humanised it.
  2. P2Islam forbade enslaving free people, encouraged releasing prisoners, required granting slaves contracts to buy their freedom, allocated zakat to freeing them, made freeing slaves the expiation for many sins, and freed a slave whose master struck him.
  3. ∴Islamic law pushed steadily against slavery.
Evidence
  • Routes in closed. Selling a free person is a major sin (Bukhari 2227). Captives may be freed as a favour or for ransom (47:4); at Badr the ransom for some literate captives was teaching Muslims to read. At the conquest of Mecca he said: "Go, you are free." Ibn Qudama: the elderly, sick, blind and monks may not be killed.
  • Routes out. Slaves who ask for a contract of freedom must be granted one (24:33). Zakat funds freeing slaves (9:60). Freeing a slave is the expiation for manslaughter (4:92), breaking oaths (5:89), and other sins, and the "steep path" to righteousness (90:13).
  • Protection. "Whoever slaps or beats his slave, his expiation is to free him" (Muslim 1657). When Abu Mas'ud struck his slave, the Prophet ﷺ said "Allah has more power over you than you have over him", and he freed him (Muslim 1659). "Feed them from what you eat and clothe them from what you wear" (Bukhari 30). Among the Prophet's last words: "the prayer, and those your right hands possess."
  • No forced intercourse. Al-Shafi'i: "no one can be compelled to it" (al-Umm). Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim held that forcing a slave woman into intercourse is a form of mutilation that frees her; al-Halimi said if she dislikes his touch he must not touch her without her permission and must sell her if she wishes. Jonathan Brown's Slavery and Islam discusses these rulings.
  • The Bible permits buying slaves from the nations as permanent property (Lev 25:44-46), allows beating a slave who survives "a day or two" without penalty because "he is his money" (Exod 21:20-21), and the NT tells slaves to obey even harsh masters (1 Pet 2:18).

Argument 5 of 8

Non-Muslims under Islamic rule

Thesis

Jews and Christians under Islam were protected, kept their courts and churches, and often welcomed Muslim rule over Byzantine persecution. Historians such as Mark Cohen find that Jews generally fared better under Islam than under medieval Christendom, though it was far from an interfaith utopia.

  1. P1A religion's treatment of minorities is judged by the historical record.
  2. P2Some Christian sources describe Arab rule as a relief from Byzantine persecution, while others are hostile; Muslims returned jizya when they could not protect; Jews fled Christian Spain to Muslim lands; and historians such as Mark Cohen find Jewish life under Islam far more secure than under Christianity.
  3. ∴Islam's record with minorities was good by the standards of its time and better than its rivals'.

  • Christian testimony. Isho'yahb III, patriarch of the Church of the East in the 7th century: the Arabs "do not persecute the Christian religion; on the contrary they favour it, honour our priests and the saints of the Lord and confer benefits on churches and monasteries."

  • Jews. Mark R. Cohen (Under Crescent and Cross) concludes that Jews under Islam had their own courts, were integrated economically, rarely faced forced conversion, and kept no collective memory of persecution comparable to Christian Europe. After 1492, Jews expelled from Spain found refuge in the Ottoman Empire and North Africa.

Argument 6 of 8

Argument 7 of 8

Hijab and modesty

Thesis

The hijab is a command to cover the head and body in public, shared with Jewish and Christian tradition, and its stated purpose is dignity and protection from harassment.

Aisha's age, 65:4 and the end of Surah at-Tawbah

4 arguments

The marriage of Aisha is the most common moral attack on the Prophet ﷺ. Some Muslims respond by arguing she was really 18 or 19; the server's scholars reject that claim as historically weak, so this section first explains why the authentic age stands, then makes the real argument: the marriage must be judged by the norms of its time, it was consummated after physical maturity, she consented and thrived, and no contemporary, not even his enemies, objected. It then answers the related objection on 65:4, and the claim that the end of Surah at-Tawbah was not reliably transmitted.

Argument 1 of 4

The authentic reports put Aisha's age at marriage at nine

Thesis

The age of six at the contract and nine at consummation is narrated through several independent chains and was agreed upon by classical scholars. The "she was 19" arguments rely on weak or misread reports.

Argument
  1. P1An honest defence must start from the authentic evidence, not the convenient one.
  2. P2The reports giving six and nine come through multiple independent chains, including one without Hisham ibn 'Urwa; Ibn Hazm, Ibn Kathir and Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reported no disagreement; the counter-arguments rely on a weak report about Asma's age, a misreading of the Uhud reports, and a confused report about Fatima.
  3. ∴Aisha was nine at consummation; the defence must address that, not deny it.
Evidence
  • Multiple chains. Bukhari 5133, 5134 and 5158 (through Hisham ibn 'Urwa); Muslim 1422 through al-A'mash from Ibrahim from al-Aswad, with no Hisham in the chain; and through Yahya ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Hatib (Abu Dawud 4937).
  • Hisham's reliability. Al-Dhahabi calls him "a proof, an imam" and rejects the claim that his memory became confused (Mizan 4/301). Ibn al-Salah and al-Nawawi note that the narrations in Bukhari and Muslim come from his sound period.
  • Agreement. Ibn Hazm said it was "without any difference"; Ibn Kathir "there is no disagreement among people"; Ibn 'Abd al-Barr "I know of no one who differed". Ahmad Shakir called claims to the contrary fabricated.
  • The counter-arguments fail. The report that Asma was ten years older comes only from Ibn Abi al-Zinad, born after Aisha's death, without a chain, and he is weak per Ibn Ma'in, Ahmad and al-Nasa'i. At Uhud, Aisha carried water as a helper, not as a combatant, so the age limit for fighters doesn't apply. The report about Fatima's age mixes two inconsistent dates. Aisha's memory of Surah al-Qamar's revelation ("I was a playful young girl, jariya") fits a girl of about four or five, and children like Ibn Abbas narrated events from age three. The idea that Arabs counted age from puberty has no basis and would make Asma 110.
Objections

"Why not accept the 19 theory? It solves the problem."

Because it is false history, and an apologetic built on weak evidence collapses as soon as an informed opponent checks it. The strong answer is the context argument below.

Practice

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

In one breath

Six at contract and nine at consummation come through several chains (Bukhari 5133; Muslim 1422 without Hisham), with classical consensus. The "19" arguments use a chainless report about Asma and misread Uhud.

Argument 2 of 4

The marriage judged by its own time, and by its outcome

Thesis

Marriage soon after puberty was normal across the ancient and medieval world, including Christian, Jewish, Persian and Roman law. The marriage was consummated after Aisha reached physical maturity, with her consent and her family's, it was never criticised by the Prophet's enemies, and she became one of the greatest scholars of Islam.

Argument
  1. P1Moral judgement of a historical act must account for the norms of its time and the actual circumstances.
  2. P2Marriage around puberty was normal everywhere; Aisha was already engaged before; the consummation was delayed until maturity; she consented; no enemy of the Prophet objected; and she flourished.
  3. ∴The marriage was not immoral in its context.
Evidence

Universal norm.

  • Sasanian Persian law expected girls to marry from nine and consummate by twelve (Encyclopaedia Iranica, "Children").
  • Roman law set the minimum age for girls at twelve; medieval canon law did the same, with betrothal from seven.
  • Geza Vermes thought Mary was likely about twelve when betrothed to Joseph; the Protevangelium of James says she was given to an elderly Joseph at twelve. Augustine's fiancée was about ten (Confessions 6.13).
  • In the 19th century, the age of consent in many US states was ten or even seven, and a few US states had no statutory minimum marriage age as recently as 2026.
  • Historians such as Neil Postman (The Disappearance of Childhood) show that "childhood" as an extended protected stage is a modern idea; Gluckman and Hanson (Mismatch) note that physical puberty and social maturity coincided historically.

Maturity.

  • Puberty varies widely: the average range is about 9-14, and medical references give menarche from 9 to 17. Hot climates are associated with earlier onset. Al-Shafi'i said he saw girls in Yemen reach puberty at nine, and a grandmother aged 21 (al-Bayhaqi).
  • Aisha said, "When a girl reaches nine, she is a woman" (Tirmidhi). She described her illness before the marriage and her hair growing back (Bukhari 3894). Jonathan Brown (Misquoting Muhammad) explains that consummation took place only after physical maturity; contract and consummation were separate stages.
  • Al-Dawudi (quoted by Qadi 'Iyad): Aisha had reached good physical maturity, and a girl who cannot bear marriage should not be given in marriage.

Consent and outcome.

  • Aisha was already engaged to Jubayr ibn Mut'im before the Prophet ﷺ proposed, through Khawla bint Hakim; her parents agreed.
  • When the Quran gave the Prophet's wives the choice to leave him (33:28-29), Aisha answered first: "I choose Allah, His Messenger and the Home of the Hereafter" (Bukhari 4785).
  • The Prophet ﷺ let a girl annul a marriage her father arranged without her consent (al-Nasa'i 3269), and Islamic law's principle "no harm" (la darar) forbids a marriage that harms the girl.
  • No objection to this marriage from the Quraysh, hypocrites or Jews of Medina is recorded in our sources (an argument from silence, so weak evidence).
  • Aisha became the leading scholar of her generation, narrating over 2,000 hadith; the Companions brought her their hardest questions.
Objections

"Morality is timeless; it was wrong then too."

Islam agrees that harm is always wrong, which is why the law forbids harmful marriages. But age norms for marriage are not timeless; they track physical maturity and social structure. If marriage at 12-14 is timelessly evil, then most of humanity's ancestors, including Mary according to Christian tradition, were in evil marriages.

"Should Muslims follow this today?"

Islamic law requires maturity, the absence of harm and, for a mature woman, her consent, and allows states to set minimum ages to prevent harm. Following the Prophet ﷺ does not mean reproducing the social age norms of 7th-century Arabia.

In one breath

Marriage around puberty was universal (Persian, Roman, canon law; Mary at twelve). Aisha's marriage was consummated after maturity, she was previously engaged, she chose the Prophet when offered a way out, no enemy objected, and she thrived as a scholar.

Argument 3 of 4

65:4: "those who have not menstruated"

Thesis

Argument
  1. P1A verse about the waiting period after divorce regulates divorce; it does not set permissions for intercourse, which are governed by other rules (maturity and no harm).
  2. P2Puberty begins well before first menstruation, and some women never menstruate or have irregular cycles; the verse gives all of them a fixed three-month period.
  3. ∴65:4 does not imply sex with prepubescent children.
Evidence
  • The verse. "Those of your women who have despaired of menstruation, if you are in doubt, their waiting period is three months, and [also for] those who have not menstruated" (65:4).

The end of Surah at-Tawbah (9:128-129)

Thesis

Argument 1 of 5

The Quran itself requires the Sunnah (against Quranists)

Thesis

The Quran refers to revelation outside its text, commands obedience to the Messenger separately, calls him its explainer, and cannot be practised without the Sunnah. Rejecting hadith therefore contradicts the Quran.

Argument
  1. P1If the Quran mentions revelations that are not in it, and makes the Prophet ﷺ the explainer of the Quran, then revelation outside the Quran exists and binds Muslims.
  2. P2The Quran refers to promises and commands not recorded in it (3:124; 2:143; 2:187), distinguishes "the Book and the Wisdom", and says the Prophet was sent "to explain to people what was sent down to them" (16:44).
  3. ∴The Sunnah is revelation alongside the Quran, and the Quranist position contradicts the Quran.
Evidence
  • Revelation not in the Quran. "Is it not sufficient that your Lord reinforces you with three thousand angels?" (3:124), a promise recorded in no earlier verse. "Allah knows you used to deceive yourselves" about intercourse at night in Ramadan (2:187), a prohibition never stated in the Quran.

  • The clarity dilemma. Quranists say the Quran is clear on its own. Yet the overwhelming majority of Muslims across 1,400 years understood it to require the Sunnah. If a "clear" book was misunderstood by almost everyone, it was not clear in the Quranist sense.

  • Ibn Taymiyya's dilemma (al-Jawab al-Sahih): Christians say Christ has two natures and two wills but is one person; yet God has one nature and one will but is three persons. By the first rule (persons follow wills), God is one person; by the second, Christ is two persons (the Nestorian position Christians condemn). The two doctrines use opposite principles.
  • Analogies fail. The sun, its rays and its heat; a man, his speech and his spirit: in each, only the first is a substance, and the others are attributes or effects, which is modalism or subordination, not three co-equal persons.
  • "Allah wonders" (37:12, in one reading, "I wondered"). Wonder from Allah implies no ignorance (Lane's Lexicon). The Bible says Jesus "marvelled" (Matt 8:10; Mark 6:6) and that YHWH "wondered that there was no one to intercede" (Isa 59:16).
  • "I am Time (al-dahr)" (Bukhari 4826). Al-Shafi'i and Ibn Qutayba explain: the Arabs cursed time for their misfortunes, but Allah is the one who turns night and day, so cursing time is cursing Him. Al-Dahr is not a name of Allah (Ibn 'Uthaymin).
  • God above. People everywhere raise their hands upward in prayer, an instinct scholars like Ibn Qudama cite as part of the fitrah; Jews and Christians also speak of God in heaven (Matt 6:9).
