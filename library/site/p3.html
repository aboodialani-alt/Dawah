Historical problems in the Old Testament

6 arguments

Christians and Jews often say that archaeology has "proved the Bible". Archaeology has confirmed that many kings, cities and peoples named in the Bible existed, but on the Bible's big founding stories it points the other way. The numbers are often impossible, the conquest stories clash with what was dug up, and some books get basic facts about foreign empires wrong. This matters for the Muslim case in two ways. It confirms the Quran's account that the earlier books were written and edited by human hands (2:79). And it lets you show that the Quran avoids these errors in the same stories, such as the size of the Exodus. The method throughout is the same: quote the verse, give the archaeological or historical evidence, and cite mainstream scholars, many of them Jewish or Christian.

Argument 1 of 6

Solomon's Temple: a huge workforce for a small building

Thesis

1 Kings lists 183,300 men (including a rotating levy of 30,000) for Solomon's building work, with seven years spent on a temple roughly 27 metres long and 9 metres wide, about the floor area of a large house. The numbers are not credible as history.

Argument
  1. P1A historical record should give numbers that fit the work described.
  2. P2The Bible gives a labour force of over 180,000 men for seven years (1 Kgs 5:13-16; 6:38) to build a stone building of 60 by 20 cubits (1 Kgs 6:2), while archaeologists estimate the population of Judah in the 10th century BCE at anywhere from a few thousand (Finkelstein) to perhaps tens of thousands.
  3. ∴The figures are exaggerated, which shows the account is not an exact historical record.
Evidence
  • The workforce. "King Solomon drafted forced labour out of all Israel, and the draft numbered 30,000 men… Solomon also had 70,000 burden-bearers and 80,000 stonecutters in the hill country, besides 3,300 chief officers" (1 Kgs 5:13-16). That is 183,300 men. 2 Chronicles 2:18 gives 3,600 officers instead of 3,300.
  • The time. "He was seven years in building it" (1 Kgs 6:38). His own palace took thirteen (7:1).
  • The building. "The house that King Solomon built for the LORD was sixty cubits long, twenty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high" (1 Kgs 6:2): about 27 by 9 metres, roughly 240 square metres of floor, plus a porch and side rooms.
  • The population. Israel Finkelstein and Neil Asher Silberman (The Bible Unearthed, 2001) estimate the population of the Judean highlands in the 10th century BCE at around 5,000 people in some twenty small villages, and Jerusalem as no more than a modest town. Even the higher estimates of other archaeologists cannot supply 183,000 labourers.
  • The gold. David is said to have set aside 100,000 talents of gold and a million talents of silver for the Temple (1 Chr 22:14). A talent was roughly 30-34 kg, so that is over 3,000 tonnes of gold, comparable to the whole world's annual gold mining today, for a small hill kingdom.
Objections

"The workers built Solomon's palace and other projects too."

The text ties the Temple work to the levy (1 Kgs 5:5, 13-18), but 1 Kgs 9:15 says the levy also built the palace, the Millo, the wall of Jerusalem, Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer. And even spread across all his building, 183,000 workers exceeds the population archaeology allows for the region.

"Archaeologists like Eilat Mazar found Solomonic structures."

Mazar's identifications are disputed, and even if accepted they show a modest administrative centre, not an empire able to field nearly 200,000 labourers and thousands of tonnes of gold.

Practice

Your opponent says

“You're relying on Finkelstein, a minimalist.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Finkelstein is a mainstream Israeli archaeologist, not a minimalist. But set his population estimate aside: 3,000 tonnes of gold for a temple in a hill kingdom is impossible on any reckoning, and Kings and Chronicles don't even agree on the number of officers.

In one breath

183,300 workers for seven years and over 3,000 tonnes of gold for a building of about 27 by 9 metres, in a region whose population archaeologists put in the thousands or low tens of thousands. The figures are not history.

Argument 2 of 6

The Exodus and the wilderness leave no trace

Thesis

The Bible describes over two million Israelites leaving Egypt and living forty years in Sinai. Egyptian records never mention it and excavations at the key wilderness sites have found nothing from that period. The Quran, by contrast, never gives the inflated numbers.

Argument
  1. P1An event on the scale described would leave archaeological or documentary traces.
  2. P2Two million people over forty years would leave camps, graves and pottery, and the loss of the slave workforce and Pharaoh's army would appear somewhere in Egyptian records. None of this has been found; Kadesh-barnea, the main base of the wandering tradition (Num 13:26; 20:1; Deut 1:46), has no remains from the right period.
  3. ∴The biblical account, at least in its numbers, is not historical; the Quran's account (a "small band", 26:54) avoids the problem.
Evidence
  • The numbers. 600,000 men on foot besides women and children (Exod 12:37); the census in Numbers 1:46 gives exactly 603,550 fighting men. With families that is two to three million people, more than the estimated population of Egypt at the time.
  • No Egyptian record. Egyptian sources mention Semitic workers and the Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BCE) mentions "Israel" as a people in Canaan, but no Egyptian text mentions a mass departure, plagues or the loss of an army.
  • The wilderness. Excavations at Kadesh-barnea (Tell el-Qudeirat) by Rudolph Cohen found no Late Bronze or Iron I settlement; the earliest fortress there dates to the 10th century BCE. Finkelstein and Silberman write that repeated surveys of Sinai have found no evidence of the wanderings.
  • Contrast with the Quran. The Quran's Pharaoh calls the Israelites "a small band" (26:54) and gives no census. See The Quran correcting earlier scripture.

إِنَّ هَٰؤُلَاءِ لَشِرْذِمَةٌ قَلِيلُونَ

26:54
Objections

"Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence."

It is, when evidence is expected and searched for. Two million people for forty years would leave far more than nomads do, and Sinai has been surveyed intensively. Muslims accept that an Exodus happened; the problem is the biblical scale and details.

"Elef means 'clan' or 'unit', not 'thousand'."

Some scholars propose this to shrink the numbers, but then Numbers 1:46's exact total of 603,550 and the arithmetic of Numbers 3:43-51 (the redemption money for 273 firstborn) make no sense. The proposal admits the plain text is unhistorical.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Muslims also believe in the Exodus, so you can't use archaeology against it.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Correct, I believe Moses led the Israelites out. The point is the Bible's version, with over two million people and exact census totals, which the evidence contradicts. The Quran's "small band" fits the evidence; the Bible's numbers don't.

In one breath

Exodus gives 603,550 men, two to three million people, for forty years in Sinai; Egyptian records are silent and Kadesh-barnea has nothing from that age. The Quran's "small band" (26:54) avoids the problem.

Argument 3 of 6

The conquest of Canaan: Jericho and Ai

Thesis

Joshua describes the dramatic destruction of Jericho and Ai. Excavation shows Jericho had no walled city in the relevant period, and Ai had been a ruin for a thousand years.

Argument
  1. P1If Joshua's conquests happened as described, the sites should show destruction at the right time.
  2. P2At Jericho, Kathleen Kenyon found the city destroyed around 1550 BCE and barely occupied afterwards; at Ai (et-Tell), Judith Marquet-Krause and Joseph Callaway found a city destroyed around 2400 BCE and abandoned until a small village around 1200 BCE.
  3. ∴The conquest stories as written do not match the archaeology.
Evidence
  • Jericho. Joshua 6 describes the walls falling and the city burned. Kathleen Kenyon's excavations (1952-58) dated the destruction of the walled city to about 1550 BCE, long before any likely date for Joshua, with only slight occupation in the 14th century and none in the 13th. John Garstang's earlier date for the walls was corrected by Kenyon.
  • Ai. Joshua 7-8 describes Ai as a city with a king and 12,000 inhabitants killed. The name Ai (ha-'Ay) means "the ruin". At et-Tell, the standard identification, excavations found a large Early Bronze city destroyed around 2400 BCE and an unwalled hamlet only around 1200 BCE. Callaway, a Southern Baptist archaeologist, concluded that the biblical account cannot be reconciled with the site.
  • Joshua vs Judges. Joshua says "Joshua took the whole land… and the land had rest from war" (Josh 11:23), while Judges 1 lists the many cities the tribes failed to conquer. See Contradictions in Old Testament stories.
  • The scholarly consensus. William Dever, Finkelstein and most archaeologists now see Israel emerging gradually from within Canaan rather than through a sudden conquest.
Objections

"Bryant Wood redated Jericho's destruction to about 1400 BCE."

Wood's redating was rejected by most archaeologists, and radiocarbon dating of the destruction layer (Bruins and van der Plicht, 1995) supported the 16th-century date.

"Ai is at a different site (Khirbet el-Maqatir)."

That is a minority proposal; most archaeologists accept et-Tell, though Maqatir is also east of Bethel and the identification is debated. Moving the site to rescue the story concedes the problem at the obvious location.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Archaeology keeps confirming the Bible.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

It confirms that places like Jericho existed. It doesn't confirm Joshua's account: Jericho's walls fell around 1550 BCE and Ai, "the ruin", lay abandoned for over a thousand years before the Iron I village, and was already a ruin before Joshua's time. Radiocarbon dating backs Kenyon.

In one breath

Kenyon dated Jericho's destruction to about 1550 BCE with no 13th-century city; Ai was a ruin from about 2400 BCE until a small village around 1200. The conquest as written does not fit the evidence.

Argument 4 of 6

Camels, Philistines and Chaldeans in the time of Abraham

Thesis

The patriarchal stories mention domesticated camels, Philistines and "Ur of the Chaldeans", all of which belong to centuries after the time the stories describe. They show the stories were written much later.

Argument
  1. P1A text that mentions things that did not yet exist in the period it describes was written after that period.
  2. P2Domesticated camels appear in the southern Levant only from about the 10th century BCE; the Philistines arrived around 1175 BCE; the Chaldeans are attested from the 9th century BCE. Genesis places all three in Abraham's time (traditionally around 2000-1800 BCE).
  3. ∴The Genesis narratives were written or edited many centuries after the events.
Evidence
  • Camels. Abraham receives camels from Pharaoh (Gen 12:16); Abraham's servant takes ten camels to find Rebekah (Gen 24:10). Lidar Sapir-Hen and Erez Ben-Yosef (Tel Aviv journal, 2013), studying camel bones in the copper mines of the Aravah, dated the introduction of domesticated camels in the region to the last third of the 10th century BCE.
  • Philistines. Abraham and Isaac deal with "Abimelech king of the Philistines" in Gerar (Gen 21:32-34; 26:1). The Philistines settled in Canaan around 1175 BCE as part of the Sea Peoples' movement. Claus Westermann (Genesis 12-36) notes the anachronism.
  • Ur of the Chaldeans. Abraham comes from "Ur of the Chaldeans" (Gen 11:31). The Chaldeans appear in Assyrian records only from the 9th century BCE.
  • "Dan". Abraham pursues his enemies "as far as Dan" (Gen 14:14), but the city received that name only after the tribe of Dan conquered it in Judges 18:29.
  • "Before any king reigned over Israel". Genesis 36:31 lists the kings of Edom "before any king reigned over the Israelites", which most critical scholars read as written after Israel had kings (traditional readers take it as anticipating the monarchy, Gen 17:6; Deut 17:14-20).
Objections

"Camels could have been used occasionally earlier."

Some wild or rare camels may have been known, but Genesis describes them as ordinary property and transport, like flocks and donkeys. The anachronism is in treating them as common.

"'Philistines' is just an updated name for the earlier people there."

Then the text was updated by later editors, which is the point: the stories as we have them were reworked centuries later.

Practice

Your opponent says

“These are tiny details; they don't affect the message.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

They show when the text was written: by people living after camels were common, after the Philistines arrived, and after Israel had kings. A text written that late can't be Moses' own record, which is what the claim of a preserved Torah requires.

In one breath

Domesticated camels (10th century BCE), Philistines (12th century BCE), Chaldeans (9th century BCE), the city of Dan, and "before any king reigned over Israel" all place Genesis' writing long after Abraham and Moses.

Argument 5 of 6

Daniel and Esther get the Persian and Babylonian world wrong

Thesis

Daniel calls Belshazzar the son of Nebuchadnezzar and king of Babylon, and invents a "Darius the Mede" who took Babylon. Esther has Mordecai exiled in 597 BCE yet serving Xerxes over a century later. These are the errors of writers far removed from the events.

Argument
  1. P1A book written by an eyewitness in the Babylonian and Persian courts would know who ruled them.
  2. P2Daniel and Esther contain errors about the identity and order of kings that cuneiform and Greek sources correct.
  3. ∴Daniel and Esther were written later than they claim, by authors who did not know the history well.
Evidence
  • Belshazzar. Daniel 5 calls Belshazzar "king" and repeatedly says Nebuchadnezzar was "his father" (5:2, 11, 13, 18). The Nabonidus Chronicle and cylinder inscriptions show Belshazzar was the son of Nabonidus, who was not Nebuchadnezzar's son (a family link through marriage is disputed), and that Belshazzar acted as regent but was never king.
  • Darius the Mede. "Darius the Mede received the kingdom, being about sixty-two years old" (Dan 5:31). No such king is known. Babylon fell to Cyrus the Persian in 539 BCE, and the Darius of history (Darius I) came later. Scholars generally see a confusion of Persian kings.
  • The date of Daniel. Most scholars date Daniel to the 160s BCE because its "predictions" are accurate up to the persecution of Antiochus IV and then go wrong about his death (Dan 11:40-45).
  • Esther. Esth 2:5-6 can be read to say Mordecai himself was carried away with Jeconiah in 597 BCE (though the Hebrew may refer to his ancestor Kish). Esther is set under Ahasuerus (Xerxes I, 486-465 BCE), which on that reading would make Mordecai well over 110 when he became prime minister. Herodotus names Xerxes' queen as Amestris, not Vashti or Esther, and Herodotus (3.84) says Persian kings were to marry only into the seven noble families, which does not fit a Jewish commoner becoming queen.
Objections

"'Father' can mean ancestor or predecessor."

It can, but Daniel 5 builds a family drama around it (the queen mother's speech, 5:10-12), and Belshazzar was not Nebuchadnezzar's descendant at all. "King" for a regent is also a stretch, yet the text has him offer the "third" rank in the kingdom because he knew he was second, which suggests the author knew he was not the true king but not why.

"Darius the Mede is another name for Cyrus or Gubaru."

These identifications are guesses made to save the text, and none is proven. Gubaru is attested in the Nabonidus Chronicle as a governor, but nothing calls him king or 'Darius'. Daniel 6:28 distinguishes "Darius" from "Cyrus the Persian".

Practice

Your opponent says

“The discovery of Belshazzar's name vindicated Daniel against sceptics.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

It showed Belshazzar existed, and that he was Nabonidus' son, not Nebuchadnezzar's, and never king. The same inscriptions are hard to square with Daniel's wording on both points.

In one breath

Belshazzar was Nabonidus' son and never king, against Daniel 5; "Darius the Mede" is unknown to history; Mordecai would be over 110 under Xerxes. These books were written well after the events.

Argument 6 of 6

Impossible numbers in Kings and Chronicles

Thesis

Chronicles and Kings give army sizes and death tolls in the hundreds of thousands, sometimes a million, for small kingdoms. They contradict each other and are impossible for the population of the time.

Argument
  1. P1Army sizes must fit the population that supplied them.
  2. P2Chronicles reports battles with a million-man army and half a million dead in a day, in kingdoms whose total populations archaeologists estimate in the low hundreds of thousands at most.
  3. ∴These numbers are legendary, not historical.
Evidence
  • Abijah vs Jeroboam. Abijah of Judah fights with 400,000 men against Jeroboam's 800,000, and "there fell slain of Israel 500,000 chosen men" (2 Chr 13:3, 17), in one battle.
  • Zerah the Cushite attacks Judah "with an army of a million men" (2 Chr 14:9).
  • The census. 2 Samuel 24:9 counts 800,000 men in Israel and 500,000 in Judah; 1 Chronicles 21:5 says 1,100,000 and 470,000.
  • Solomon's horses. 40,000 stalls (1 Kgs 4:26) or 4,000 (2 Chr 9:25).
  • For scale. The largest armies of the great empires of the day, such as Assyria, are recorded at tens of thousands in their own inscriptions. Shalmaneser III boasts of mustering 120,000 troops for a campaign in 845 BCE, one of the largest numbers in the ancient Near East, and historians treat even that as inflated.
Objections

"These are copying errors in numbers."

Then the Bible's numbers were corrupted in transmission, which is the Quran's point. And the 2 Chronicles figures are consistent with each other (400,000, 800,000, 500,000), suggesting deliberate exaggeration rather than one slip.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Numbers in ancient texts are often symbolic; you're reading them too literally.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Then you concede the history isn't literal. But these aren't round symbolic figures in poetry; they're census totals and battle reports, and Samuel and Chronicles give different census totals (800,000 vs 1,100,000).

In one breath

500,000 killed in one battle, a million-man army, census totals that differ between books, and 40,000 vs 4,000 stalls. Even Assyria's largest recorded armies were a fraction of these numbers.

Contradictions in Old Testament stories

6 arguments

Many Old Testament stories contain two versions of the same event stitched together: two plans to save Joseph, two sets of traders who sell him, two creation orders, two counts of the animals in the ark, two killers of Goliath, two deaths of Saul. Modern scholarship explains this by the Torah and the historical books being compiled from earlier sources that told the story differently (the "documentary hypothesis" and its successors). For a Muslim this is useful for two reasons. It shows the text is a human compilation, as the Quran says (2:79). And the Quran itself sets the test: "Do they not reflect on the Quran? Had it been from other than Allah, they would have found in it much contradiction" (4:82). Where the Quran tells the same story, such as Joseph's, it tells one consistent version.

Argument 1 of 6

Joseph sold by Ishmaelites or Midianites? (Genesis 37)

Thesis

Genesis 37 combines two accounts: in one, Judah has the brothers sell Joseph to Ishmaelites; in the other, Reuben plans to rescue him and Midianites take him from the pit without the brothers knowing. The two are woven together and contradict each other.

Argument
  1. P1A single, coherent account of an event cannot have two different groups take the same person, with the brothers both selling him and not knowing what happened to him.
  2. P2Genesis 37 has Judah propose selling Joseph to Ishmaelites (37:27), Midianites pull him from the pit (37:28), Reuben find the pit empty and be shocked (37:29-30), the Midianites sell him to Potiphar (37:36), and then Potiphar buy him from the Ishmaelites (39:1).
  3. ∴The chapter merges two incompatible versions, which shows it is an edited compilation.
Evidence

The text in order.

  • The brothers throw Joseph into an empty pit, on Reuben's advice: Reuben intended "to rescue him out of their hand to restore him to his father" (37:21-22).
  • They sit down to eat and see "a caravan of Ishmaelites coming from Gilead" (37:25).
  • Judah proposes: "Come, let us sell him to the Ishmaelites" (37:27). The brothers agree.
  • "Then Midianite traders passed by. And they drew Joseph up and lifted him out of the pit, and sold him to the Ishmaelites for twenty shekels of silver" (37:28).
  • "When Reuben returned to the pit and saw that Joseph was not in the pit, he tore his clothes and said: the boy is gone, and I, where shall I go?" (37:29-30). Reuben apparently knows nothing of a sale; the text says he 'returned to the pit', so he was away at the time.
  • "Meanwhile the Midianites [Hebrew: Medanites] had sold him in Egypt to Potiphar" (37:36).
  • "Potiphar… bought him from the Ishmaelites who had brought him down there" (39:1).
  • Yet later Joseph tells his brothers "you sold me here" (45:4-5).

The two stories. Read separately, one version runs: Judah, Ishmaelites, sale by the brothers, Ishmaelites sell to Potiphar (37:25-27, 28b; 39:1). The other: Reuben, the pit, Midianites take him unseen, Reuben's shock, Midianites sell to Potiphar (37:22-24, 28a, 29-30, 36). Richard Friedman (Who Wrote the Bible?) and most source critics assign these to the J and E sources. Even traditional Jewish commentators (Rashi) had to suggest Joseph was sold several times to make the text work.

The Quran's version. One brother proposes throwing him into the well so that travellers pick him up (12:10). A caravan comes, its water-drawer finds him, "and they sold him for a cheap price, a few dirhams" (12:19-20). One story, no contradiction.

Objections

"'Midianites' and 'Ishmaelites' are the same group; Judges 8:24 calls Midianites Ishmaelites."

In Genesis they descend from different sons of Abraham, but Judges 8:24 shows the names could overlap, so the argument rests on 37:28-36 reading as two groups. Ishmael is Abraham's son by Hagar (Gen 16) and Midian his son by Keturah (Gen 25:2). And in 37:28 the Midianites sell him to the Ishmaelites, which makes no sense if they are the same people.

"'They' in 37:28 means the brothers drew him up and sold him."

The nearest antecedent is the Midianites, and if the brothers sold him, Reuben's shock at the empty pit (37:29-30) makes no sense.

Practice

Your opponent says

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

The problem isn't the destination; it's who did what. In one strand the brothers sell him; in the other he is taken without their knowledge and Reuben is shocked to find the pit empty. Both can't be true, and 37:36 and 39:1 even disagree about who sold him to Potiphar.

In one breath

Judah proposes selling to Ishmaelites; Midianites pull him out; Reuben finds the pit empty and is shocked; Midianites sell him to Potiphar, who buys him from Ishmaelites. Two stories merged. The Quran tells one coherent account (12:10-20).

Argument 2 of 6

Two creation stories in a different order (Genesis 1 and 2)

Thesis

Genesis 1 creates plants, then animals, then man and woman together. Genesis 2 creates man first, then plants, then animals, then woman. The order of creation is reversed.

Argument
  1. P1One account of a single creation cannot give two different orders of events.
  2. P2Genesis 1 puts plants on day 3, animals on days 5-6 and humans (male and female together) last; Genesis 2 forms the man before any plant has sprung up, then plants a garden, then forms the animals to find him a partner, then makes the woman.
  3. ∴Genesis 1 and 2 are two different creation accounts placed side by side.
Evidence
  • Genesis 1. Vegetation on day 3 (1:11-13), birds and fish on day 5 (1:20-23), land animals on day 6 (1:24-25), then "God created man… male and female He created them" (1:27).
  • Genesis 2. "When no bush of the field was yet in the land and no small plant had yet sprung up… the LORD God formed the man" (2:5-7). Then "the LORD God planted a garden" (2:8-9). Then "it is not good that the man should be alone… so out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field and every bird" and brought them to the man (2:18-19). Finally the woman is made from his rib (2:21-22).
  • Style. Genesis 1 uses Elohim (God) and a repetitive day structure; Genesis 2 uses YHWH Elohim (the LORD God) and a story form. Scholars assign Genesis 1:1-2:3 to the Priestly source and 2:4b onward to the Yahwist.
Objections

"Genesis 2:19 means 'had formed' the animals earlier (pluperfect)."

Some translations (NIV) render it that way, but the Hebrew is the normal narrative form, and the context ("it is not good for the man to be alone… so the LORD God formed…") presents the animals as made in response to the man's loneliness.

"Genesis 2 is about the garden, not the whole creation."

It begins "when no bush of the field was yet in the land" (2:5), describing the world before plants, and has man formed before them.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Genesis 2 is a close-up of day six, not a second account.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Then man would be created after the plants, but Genesis 2:5-7 says he was formed when no plant had yet sprung up. And the animals are made after him to find him a helper (2:18-19), while Genesis 1 makes them before him.

In one breath

Genesis 1: plants, animals, then man and woman together. Genesis 2: man before plants, then animals, then woman. Different divine names and styles mark two sources.

Argument 3 of 6

The Flood: how many animals, how long?

Thesis

The Flood story gives two different instructions about the animals and two different timescales, again showing two sources combined.

Argument
  1. P1A single account of one flood cannot command both two of every kind and seven pairs of clean animals, or give two durations.
  2. P2Genesis 6:19-20 commands one pair of every kind; Genesis 7:2-3 commands seven pairs of clean animals and birds; 7:12 and 7:17 give forty days, while 7:24 and 8:3 give 150 days.
  3. ∴The Flood narrative combines two versions.
Evidence
  • Animals. "Of every living thing… you shall bring two of every sort into the ark" (6:19). "Take with you seven pairs of all clean animals… and a pair of the animals that are not clean, and seven pairs of the birds" (7:2-3). Then "two and two… went into the ark with Noah" (7:9).
  • Duration. "Rain fell upon the earth forty days and forty nights" (7:12); "the flood continued forty days on the earth" (7:17). But "the waters prevailed on the earth 150 days" (7:24) and receded "at the end of 150 days" (8:3).
  • Birds. Noah sends out a raven (8:7) and then a dove three times (8:8-12), which many scholars read as two versions of the same scene.
  • Scholarship. The Flood story is a textbook example of interwoven Yahwist (seven pairs, forty days) and Priestly (one pair, 150 days) sources.
  • The Quran's version is short and consistent: "Load into it of every kind a pair" (11:40; 23:27).
Arabic

قُلْنَا احْمِلْ فِيهَا مِن كُلٍّ زَوْجَيْنِ اثْنَيْنِ

11:40
Objections

"Seven pairs of clean animals is just an extra detail, for sacrifice later."

The first command is "two of every sort" with no exception; and the clean/unclean distinction is given in the Law at Sinai, centuries later in the story.

"Forty days of rain, then 150 days of high water."

That's a possible harmonisation of the timing, but the two counts come from different strands, and the animal instructions still conflict.

6:19 says two of every living thing; 7:2 says seven pairs of clean ones. And 7:9 then says they went in "two and two". The text isn't refining one command; it's holding two.

Two of every kind (6:19) or seven pairs of the clean (7:2); forty days (7:17) or 150 (7:24); a raven and a dove. The Quran says simply "a pair of every kind" (11:40).

Argument 4 of 6

Who killed Goliath? How did Saul die? When did David meet Saul?

Thesis

The books of Samuel give two killers of Goliath, two first meetings of David and Saul, and several conflicting accounts of Saul's death; Chronicles then rewrites some of them.

Argument
  1. P1A reliable history does not credit the same act to two different people or give two incompatible first meetings.
  2. P21 Samuel 17 has David kill Goliath; 2 Samuel 21:19 has Elhanan kill him. David becomes Saul's beloved armour-bearer in 1 Samuel 16, but Saul does not know who he is in 17:55-58. Saul kills himself (1 Sam 31), is killed by an Amalekite (2 Sam 1), by the Philistines (2 Sam 21:12), or by the LORD (1 Chr 10:14).
  3. ∴These books combine conflicting traditions.
Evidence
  • Goliath. David kills "Goliath of Gath" (1 Sam 17:4, 50). But "Elhanan the son of Jaare-oregim, the Bethlehemite, struck down Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam" (2 Sam 21:19), the same description as 1 Samuel 17:7. Chronicles later says Elhanan killed "Lahmi the brother of Goliath" (1 Chr 20:5), which scholars see as a harmonisation (Lahmi looks like a corruption of Beit-ha-Lahmi, "the Bethlehemite"). The KJV adds "the brother of" in 2 Samuel 21:19 in italics, admitting it is not in the Hebrew.
  • David and Saul. In 1 Samuel 16:18-23 David is brought to Saul as a musician; "Saul loved him greatly, and he became his armour-bearer", and Saul sends word to Jesse. In 17:55-58, after Goliath, Saul asks "whose son is this youth?" and Abner says he doesn't know. Notably, the oldest Greek text (Septuagint, Codex Vaticanus) lacks 17:12-31 and 17:55-18:5, suggesting to many scholars that the second version may be a later addition to the Hebrew (others think the Greek omitted it to harmonise).
  • Saul's death.
    • Saul "took his own sword and fell upon it" (1 Sam 31:4).
    • An Amalekite says: "I stood beside him and killed him" (2 Sam 1:10).
    • "The Philistines had struck down Saul on Gilboa" (2 Sam 21:12), a passing remark that is compatible with 1 Sam 31 on a loose reading.
    • "He did not seek guidance from the LORD. Therefore the LORD put him to death" (1 Chr 10:13-14), though 1 Samuel 28:6 says Saul did inquire of the LORD, "and the LORD did not answer him".
Objections

"The Amalekite lied to win David's favour."

Possible for that one, and many Christians argue it. But the Goliath and first-meeting problems remain, and Chronicles' claim that Saul did not inquire of the LORD directly contradicts 1 Samuel 28:6.

"There were two Goliaths."

2 Samuel 21:19 describes the same man, from the same city, with the same weaver's-beam spear. Chronicles' change to "brother of Goliath" is itself the evidence that later writers saw the problem.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Chronicles clarifies that Elhanan killed Goliath's brother.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Chronicles was written centuries after Samuel and changes "the Bethlehemite" into a name, "Lahmi". That is a later scribe smoothing over a contradiction, which is exactly the editing the Quran describes. Even the KJV prints "the brother of" in italics because it isn't in the Hebrew.

In one breath

David or Elhanan killed Goliath; Saul knew David as his beloved armour-bearer yet didn't know whose son he was; Saul died by his own sword, by an Amalekite, by the Philistines or by the LORD. The Septuagint lacks the second David-Saul story.

Argument 5 of 6

More contradictions in names, ages and events

Thesis

Kings, Chronicles and the Pentateuch disagree on names, ages and whether enemies were wiped out. Some of the ages are impossible.

Argument
  1. P1Contradictory facts about the same person or event cannot both be true.
  2. P2The Old Testament gives contradictory ages, names and outcomes in parallel accounts.
  3. ∴The text contains errors, whether from authors or scribes.
Evidence
  • Ahaziah's age. 22 when he began to reign (2 Kgs 8:26) or 42 (2 Chr 22:2 in Hebrew). At 42 he would be two years older than his father Jehoram, who died at 40 (2 Chr 21:20).
  • Jehoiachin. 18 when he began to reign (2 Kgs 24:8) or 8 (2 Chr 36:9) in the Masoretic reading (some manuscripts have 18). Chronicles, like Kings (2 Kgs 24:9), says he "did what was evil in the sight of the LORD".
  • Who incited the census? "The anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and He incited David" (2 Sam 24:1), versus "Satan stood against Israel and incited David" (1 Chr 21:1).
  • Moses' father-in-law is called Reuel (Exod 2:18) and Jethro (Exod 3:1); Judg 4:11 calls Hobab his father-in-law, though Num 10:29 makes Hobab Reuel's son.
  • Esau's wives are Judith, Basemath daughter of Elon, and Mahalath daughter of Ishmael (Gen 26:34; 28:9), or Adah daughter of Elon, Oholibamah, and Basemath daughter of Ishmael (Gen 36:2-3).
  • Ishmael's age. Ishmael is 14 when Isaac is born (Gen 16:16; 21:5), so about 16 or 17 when Hagar is sent away; yet Hagar carries "the child" and casts him under a bush, and the angel tells her to "lift up the boy and hold him by the hand" (Gen 21:14-18), as with an infant.
  • Destroyed enemies return. Moses' army kills every male Midianite (Num 31:7); a few generations later Midianites "as numerous as locusts" oppress Israel for seven years (Judg 6:1-5). Saul "utterly destroyed all the people" of Amalek (1 Sam 15:8); a few chapters later the Amalekites raid Ziklag and 400 of them escape David on camels (1 Sam 30:1, 17).
  • The conquest. "Joshua took the whole land" (Josh 11:23); but Judges 1 lists the cities the tribes failed to take. Hebron is captured by Joshua (Josh 10:36-37), by Caleb (Josh 15:13-14) and by Judah (Judg 1:10). Jerusalem's Jebusites could not be driven out (Josh 15:63), yet Judah "captured Jerusalem and set the city on fire" (Judg 1:8).
  • Seeing God. "You cannot see My face, for man shall not see Me and live" (Exod 33:20), yet "the LORD used to speak to Moses face to face" (33:11), Jacob says "I have seen God face to face" (Gen 32:30), and Moses and the elders "saw the God of Israel" and ate (Exod 24:10-11).
  • The Sabbath's reason. In Exodus the Sabbath commemorates creation (Exod 20:11); in Deuteronomy's version of the same commandment, the exodus from Egypt (Deut 5:15).
Objections

"These are copyist errors in numbers, not errors in the originals."

Then the text we have was corrupted in transmission, which is the Muslim position; and many of these (Midianites, Amalekites, Saul's death, the conquest) are narrative contradictions, not numbers.

"'Face to face' is idiomatic for intimacy."

Then Exodus 33:11 and 33:20 use the same "face" language in opposite senses within ten verses, and Exodus 24:10 says they "saw the God of Israel" with a description of His feet.

Practice

Your opponent says

“Every apparent contradiction has been answered by scholars like Gleason Archer.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Archer's harmonisations often require assumptions the text doesn't make, like two Goliaths or a Jehoiachin who was co-regent at 8. Chronicles itself had to rewrite Samuel; the KJV prints its fix in italics. When the Bible's own later books and translators patch the earlier ones, the contradictions are real.

In one breath

Ahaziah 22 or 42; Jehoiachin 18 or 8; the LORD or Satan incites David; three names for Moses' father-in-law; Midianites and Amalekites wiped out then back in force; Joshua took all the land, or didn't. Compare the Quran's challenge in 4:82.

Argument 6 of 6

What the contradictions show

Thesis

The doublets and contradictions point to a text compiled from earlier sources and edited over centuries. That is exactly what the Quran says happened to the earlier scriptures, and it is the opposite of what the Quran claims for itself.

Argument
  1. P1A text that combines incompatible versions of the same events was compiled by human editors, not received in a single revelation.
  2. P2The Torah and historical books do this repeatedly, and mainstream scholarship explains the Torah as a combination of sources (J, E, P, D), with Chronicles rewriting Samuel and Kings.
  3. P3The Quran says a book from other than Allah would contain much contradiction (4:82) and that some of the People of the Book wrote with their own hands and attributed it to God (2:79).
  4. ∴The Old Testament as we have it bears the marks of human compilation, as the Quran says; the Quran presents itself as a single, consistent revelation that tells these stories without such contradictions.
Evidence
  • The documentary hypothesis, developed by Julius Wellhausen and refined by later scholars (Richard Friedman, Who Wrote the Bible?; Joel Baden, The Composition of the Pentateuch), explains the doublets by sources with different names for God, styles and concerns.
  • Chronicles (around the 4th century BCE) reworks Samuel-Kings, omitting David's sin with Bathsheba and changing details such as Goliath's killer and the census instigator.
  • Jeremiah 8:8 already complains of "the lying pen of the scribes" (see The Quran correcting earlier scripture).
  • The Quran's stories of Joseph, Noah, Moses and Saul (Talut) do not contain Genesis-style contradictory accounts within a single narrative (the Quran does retell stories with differing emphasis); where they differ from the Bible they remove the problem (a "small band", one sale of Joseph).
Arabic

فَوَيْلٌ لِّلَّذِينَ يَكْتُبُونَ الْكِتَابَ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ ثُمَّ يَقُولُونَ هَٰذَا مِنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ

2:79
Objections

"The documentary hypothesis is outdated."

The specific four-source model has been revised, but the conclusion that the Pentateuch is composite and edited is accepted across critical scholarship, Jewish and Christian. Alternatives (supplementary or fragmentary models) agree it was compiled over time.

Practice

Your opponent says

“If the Bible contains contradictions, so does the Quran; critics have lists of them too.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Those lists are answered verse by verse (see Objections to the Quran), and none is a doublet like Genesis 37, where one chapter tells two incompatible versions of the same event. The Quran challenges readers to find contradictions (4:82); the Bible's own later books had to rewrite its earlier ones.

In one breath

The doublets show the Old Testament was compiled from sources and edited over centuries, as scholars from Wellhausen to Friedman and Baden explain and as the Quran says (2:79). The Quran's own versions don't contain these contradictions (4:82).

Judaism & the Talmud

4 arguments

The Quran addresses the Jews of Medina directly, and many of its statements about them match rabbinic literature so closely that they show knowledge of Jewish texts no unlettered Arab could have had. This section argues that the Quran engages accurately with rabbinic claims, that Jewish thought itself allows for a later prophet to change the Law, that leading rabbis recognised Muslims as true monotheists, and that the Torah's own textual history points beyond Moses.

Argument 1 of 4

The Quran answers rabbinic teachings precisely

Thesis

The Quran quotes or answers specific rabbinic sayings: the Mishnah on saving a soul, the claim that all Israel has a share in the next world, the claim that Jews will be in Hell only briefly, the authority of the rabbis, and God's "chained hand".

Argument
  1. P1If the Quran accurately reflects specific rabbinic texts unavailable in Arabic, its author had knowledge beyond an unlettered Meccan.
  2. P2The Quran's statements in 5:32, 2:80, 3:24, 9:31 and 5:64 correspond to identifiable passages in the Mishnah, Talmud and Jewish liturgy, sometimes in a corrected form.
  3. ∴The Quran shows informed, critical knowledge of Jewish tradition, consistent with its claim to come from the God who revealed the Torah.
Evidence
  • Saving one soul (5:32). "We decreed for the Children of Israel that whoever kills a soul… it is as if he had killed all mankind; and whoever saves one, it is as if he had saved all mankind." The Mishnah (Sanhedrin 4:5) has a similar saying, that whoever destroys one soul is treated as if he destroyed a whole world, in the context of the creation of Adam. The printed versions read "whoever destroys a single soul of Israel", but the oldest manuscripts (such as the Kaufmann manuscript) lack "of Israel", and scholars regard it as a later addition. The Quran cites the universal form, says it was "decreed for the Children of Israel", which is exactly where it is found.
  • "All Israel has a share in the world to come" (Mishnah Sanhedrin 10:1). The Quran answers: "those who sell Allah's covenant… will have no share in the Hereafter" (3:77), and "say: if the home of the Hereafter is for you alone… then wish for death" (2:94).
  • "The Fire will touch us only for a few days" (2:80; 3:24). The Talmud (Eruvin 19a) says Abraham sits at the gate of Gehinnom and rescues circumcised Israelites, and the Mishnah (Eduyot 2:10) limits the judgment of the wicked in Gehinnom to twelve months. José Costa has studied the parallels.
  • "They took their rabbis as lords besides Allah" (9:31). The Mishnah says "let the fear of your teacher be as the fear of Heaven" (Avot 4:12), and the Talmud says "a sage is greater than a prophet" (Bava Batra 12a). In the famous story of the oven of Akhnai (Bava Metzia 59b), the rabbis overrule a heavenly voice, and God is said to laugh: "My children have defeated Me." The Prophet ﷺ explained 9:31 to 'Adi ibn Hatim: they obeyed their scholars in making lawful what God forbade and forbidding what He made lawful (Tirmidhi 3095).
  • "Allah's hand is chained" (5:64). The liturgical poet Eleazar Kalir's laments for Tisha B'Av describe God's right hand held back or bound in exile, which one scholar (Shari Lowin) proposes lies behind Quran 5:64, based on Lamentations 2:3, and the Talmud (Berakhot 3a) has God roaring and lamenting over the destruction.
Arabic

مِنْ أَجْلِ ذَٰلِكَ كَتَبْنَا عَلَىٰ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ أَنَّهُ مَن قَتَلَ نَفْسًا بِغَيْرِ نَفْسٍ أَوْ فَسَادٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَكَأَنَّمَا قَتَلَ النَّاسَ جَمِيعًا

5:32

اتَّخَذُوا أَحْبَارَهُمْ وَرُهْبَانَهُمْ أَرْبَابًا مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ

9:31
Objections

"This shows Muhammad copied from Jews."

Copying would reproduce the texts; the Quran corrects them (a universal soul, no guaranteed share in the next world, no limited Hell, no human legislators over God). And the rabbinic material was in Hebrew and Aramaic, in academies in Babylonia and Palestine, not in Mecca.

Practice

Your opponent says

“5:32 copies the Talmud, so the Quran isn't revelation.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

The Quran says "We decreed this for the Children of Israel". It is telling you the ruling is in their tradition; that's a citation, not plagiarism. And it gives the universal version found in the oldest Mishnah manuscripts, not the later "of Israel".

In one breath

The Quran cites the Mishnah's "whoever saves a soul" in its older universal form, answers "all Israel has a share", the limited-Hell belief, the rabbis' authority and the "bound hand" laments. It knows rabbinic tradition and corrects it.

Argument 2 of 4

Jewish thought allows a later prophet to change the Law

Thesis

Jews argue the Torah is eternal and cannot be replaced. But the Torah's "forever" is conditional, the prophets foretold a new covenant and a new Torah, and Jewish thinkers such as Joseph Albo admitted a later prophet could change commandments.

Argument
  1. P1If "forever" in the Torah is used for things that ended, and Jewish sources speak of a new Torah and new covenant, then the eternity of the Mosaic Law is not a biblical certainty.
  2. P2Eternal promises like Eli's priesthood were revoked; Jeremiah promises a new covenant; Isaiah 51:4 says "a Torah will go forth from Me"; rabbinic midrash speaks of festivals being abolished; Joseph Albo allowed for abrogation.
  3. ∴Islam's claim that the final revelation updates the Law is consistent with Jewish scripture and thought.
Evidence
  • Conditional "forever". God promised Eli's house the priesthood "forever", then said: "far be it from Me… those who despise Me shall be lightly esteemed" (1 Sam 2:30). The Torah is a covenant (Exod 24:7) with conditions (Deut 28). 'Olam can mean "for a long time": a slave who stays serves his master "forever" (le'olam, Exod 21:6), meaning for life.
  • New covenant. "Behold, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel… not like the covenant that I made with their fathers" (Jer 31:31-32).
  • A new Torah. "A Torah shall go forth from Me" (Isa 51:4). The midrash (Vayikra Rabbah 13:3, Rabbi Avin) reads it as "a new Torah will go forth from Me", which Jewish tradition usually takes as new teaching of the same Torah. Another midrash says that in the future all the festivals will be abolished except Purim (and Yom Kippur).
  • Joseph Albo (Sefer ha-Ikkarim, book 3, 15th century) held that it is possible in principle for a divine law to be replaced, since a law may be given to suit the people at a particular time, but that the Torah of Moses will not in fact be replaced.
  • Ezekiel contradicts the Torah on details of sacrifice so seriously that the Talmud says his book was nearly withdrawn until Hananiah ben Hezekiah reconciled it (Shabbat 13b; Menachot 45a).
Arabic

الَّذِينَ يَتَّبِعُونَ الرَّسُولَ النَّبِيَّ الْأُمِّيَّ الَّذِي يَجِدُونَهُ مَكْتُوبًا عِندَهُمْ فِي التَّوْرَاةِ وَالْإِنجِيلِ

7:157
Objections

"Maimonides' ninth principle says the Torah will never be changed."

Maimonides' principles are a medieval creed, and Albo, a major Jewish philosopher, disagreed with parts of them. The question is what the Bible says, and the Bible has conditional eternities, a new covenant and a new Torah.

Practice

Your opponent says

“God's Law is eternal; a God who changes His law is fickle.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

The Torah itself changed things: Jacob married two sisters, which Leviticus 18:18 later forbids; Abraham's descendants ate what Moses later forbade. Adapting law to a people's circumstances is wisdom, not fickleness, and Jeremiah 31:31 announces a new covenant.

In one breath

Eli's "forever" priesthood was revoked, Jeremiah promises a new covenant, the midrash reads Isaiah 51:4 as "a new Torah", and Joseph Albo allowed a later prophet to change commandments. Abrogation is not foreign to Jewish thought.

Argument 3 of 4

Rabbis recognised Muslims as true monotheists, and prophets outside Israel

Thesis

Maimonides ruled that Muslims are not idolaters and affirm God's unity correctly, and Jewish tradition recognises prophets from outside Israel, such as Job and Balaam.

Argument
  1. P1If Judaism's greatest legal authority affirmed Islam's monotheism and Judaism accepts non-Israelite prophets, then Jews cannot dismiss Muhammad ﷺ simply because he was not an Israelite, or Islam as idolatry.
  2. P2Maimonides ruled exactly that, and the Bible and rabbis recognise non-Israelite prophets.
  3. ∴A Jew's objection must be to Muhammad's specific message, not to the possibility of an Arab prophet; and Islam's monotheism is the kind Maimonides ruled to be a proper unity, though he rejected Muhammad's prophethood.
Evidence
  • Maimonides (12th century), in a responsum to Obadiah the proselyte: "The Ishmaelites are not idolaters at all… they affirm the unity of God with a proper unity, in which there is no defect." He contrasted this with Christianity, which he regarded as idolatrous.
  • Non-Israelite prophets. Some rabbis (Bava Batra 15a-b) held Job to be a gentile prophet; others disagreed; Balaam prophesied (Num 22-24); the rabbis say there were prophets among the nations. The Torah promises Ishmael a great nation (Gen 17:20; 21:13, 18).
  • "A prophet like you from among their brothers" (Deut 18:18). Gen 16:12 and 25:18 speak of Ishmael dwelling 'over against all his brothers', and Muslim readers take 'their brothers' in Deut 18:18 as including Ishmael's line (Jews read it as Israelites, cf. Deut 17:15).
Objections

"Maimonides also called Muhammad a madman."

He did, in the Epistle to Yemen. But his legal ruling that Muslim worship is true monotheism stands, and it is that ruling, not his polemic, that bears on whether Islam's God is the God of Israel.

Practice

Your opponent says

“God's covenant is only with Israel; there can't be an Arab prophet.”

The Torah promises Ishmael a great nation, Job and Balaam were non-Israelite recipients of revelation, and Maimonides ruled that Muslims worship the one God with a unity "in which there is no defect".

In one breath

Maimonides ruled that Muslims affirm God's unity without defect; Jewish tradition accepts non-Israelite prophets like Job and Balaam; and the Samaritan Torah's Deuteronomy 34:10 looks forward to a coming prophet like Moses.

Argument 4 of 4

The Torah's own text: authorship, variants and edits

Thesis

The Torah was not transmitted as a single fixed text from Moses: the book of the law was found under Josiah, the Samaritan and Masoretic versions differ on theological points, and the Samaritan text removes anthropomorphisms.

Argument
  1. P1If the Torah exists in rival versions with theological differences, it has been edited by its transmitters.
  2. P2The Samaritan Pentateuch and Masoretic Text differ in several thousand places, a few dozen of them theologically significant, including where God's chosen place is and how God is described.
  3. ∴The Torah's text was shaped by its communities, confirming the Quran's description (2:79; 4:46).
Evidence
  • Found, not transmitted. The book of the law was "found" in the Temple under Josiah after generations of neglect (2 Kgs 22:8; see The Quran correcting earlier scripture).
  • Samaritan vs Masoretic. The Samaritan Pentateuch removes or softens anthropomorphic expressions (for example in Exod 15:8 and Deut 32:6), and inserts a command to build the altar on Mount Gerizim into the Ten Commandments. Each community shaped the text toward its own theology (Anderson and Giles).
  • Deuteronomy 34, describing Moses' death and saying "no prophet has arisen since in Israel like Moses", was obviously written after him.
  • Mark Smith and others trace how the God of Israel absorbed the titles of El and features of Baal, and early Egyptian texts mention "YHW" in the land of the Shasu nomads of Edom/Midian.
Objections

"The Masoretic Text is the faithful one; the Samaritans changed theirs."

Both show changes; the Dead Sea Scrolls preserve a different reading at Deut 32:8 ("sons of God"), which most scholars regard as older than the MT's "sons of Israel". The point is not which is better but that the Torah was not preserved as one untouched text.

Practice

Your opponent says

“The Torah has been copied letter by letter for 3,000 years.”

Answer it in your head first, then compare.

Our oldest full Masoretic manuscripts are from around 1000 CE; the Dead Sea Scrolls show several text forms circulating in the same period; and the Samaritan version differs from it in thousands of places. Letter-by-letter copying came late, after the text was already shaped.

In one breath

The book of the law was found under Josiah; the Samaritan and Masoretic Torahs differ theologically; Deuteronomy 34 was written after Moses. The Torah was shaped by its transmitters, as the Quran says.
